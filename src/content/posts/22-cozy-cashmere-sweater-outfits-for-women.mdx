## 1. The Grey Cashmere Crewneck with Straight Jeans

A grey cashmere crewneck with dark straight jeans is the outfit you will reach for most this winter, because it looks polished with almost no effort. The crewneck should be slightly oversized — not boxy, just relaxed through the body — and the jeans should be a dark wash with a straight leg. Tuck the front in so the waistline shows.

White sneakers and a tan leather tote keep it daytime-ready, and a thin gold chain is the only jewellery you need. Grey cashmere is the most forgiving colour: it hides the odd mark, pairs with every other neutral, and looks expensive without trying.

Formula: grey cashmere crewneck + dark straight jeans + white sneakers + tan leather tote.

Tip: cashmere pills where bags rub, so carry a tote on the opposite shoulder from the strap, or switch to a crossbody worn over a coat.

### Your Cashmere Buying and Care Checklist

Before you buy, or before you wear the piece you already own, run through this list:

- **100% cashmere or a high blend** — a tight knit and longer fibres pill less and last longer.
- **A classic shape first** — a crewneck or turtleneck in a neutral colour will outlast any trend piece.
- **Fine-gauge for layering** — chunky knits are for wearing alone, fine knits go under coats and blazers.
- **Hand-wash cold, dry flat** — never wring cashmere and never hang it wet, or the shoulders stretch.
- **A fabric shaver in the drawer** — a quick pass once a week keeps pilling under control.
- **Fold, don't hang** — hanging stretches cashmere shoulders out of shape within a season.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is cashmere worth the money?

Yes, if you buy the right piece — a classic crewneck or turtleneck in a neutral colour will last years and cost less per wear than three cheap sweaters that pill and lose shape. Look for 100% cashmere or a high cashmere blend, a tight knit, and a reputable brand. Budget options from Uniqlo and Quince are good starting points, while investment pieces from J.Crew or Naadam last longer. ### How do I stop cashmere from pilling?

Pilling happens where fabric rubs — under bag straps, at the elbows, and at the waistband. Carry a bag on the opposite shoulder, avoid rough fabrics like tweed against cashmere, and use a fabric shaver or cashmere comb weekly. Higher-quality, longer-fibre cashmere pills less, and pilling usually slows down after the first few wears. ### Can I wash cashmere at home?

Yes — hand-wash in cold water with a gentle detergent, press the water out without wringing, and lay the sweater flat to dry. Never hang a wet cashmere sweater, or the shoulders will stretch out of shape. Dry-cleaning is fine but unnecessary for every wash; hand-washing is gentler on the fibres. Always check the label first, since some blends need dry-cleaning.

### What colour cashmere should I buy first?

Buy grey if you want the most versatile piece, camel if you wear mostly denim and cream, or black if you wear mostly dark neutrals. Grey hides marks and pairs with every colour, camel warms up a winter wardrobe, and black layers under everything. Pick a shade you already own scarves and bags in, so the sweater joins your wardrobe instead of starting a new one.

### How do I layer cashmere without looking bulky?

Choose fine-gauge cashmere for layering under coats and blazers, and save chunky knits for wearing on their own. Tuck the sweater in loosely so the waistline reads, and keep the layers underneath thin — a fitted T-shirt or a camisole, never a thick shirt. A coat one size up from your usual will close comfortably over a cashmere sweater.

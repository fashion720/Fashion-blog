## Fabric Painting Tips

Wash and dry your jeans before painting to remove any factory finish that could stop the paint from bonding properly. Place cardboard or wax paper inside each leg to prevent bleed-through while you work. Use fabric-specific paint or a fabric medium mixed with acrylic paint, and always heat-set the design with an iron (through a cloth barrier) once fully dry to keep it from cracking or fading in the wash. Start with painter's tape for clean lines if freehand isn't your strength.

## Senior Jeans Painting Questions, Answered

Q1: What kind of paint works best on denim?\
A1: Fabric paint or acrylic paint mixed with a fabric medium both work well on denim. Fabric paint tends to stay more flexible after washing, while acrylic with medium offers a slightly wider color range.

Q2: How do you keep painted jeans from cracking or fading?\
A2: Heat-set the design with an iron through a protective cloth once the paint is fully dry, and always wash the finished jeans inside out on a gentle, cold cycle to protect the design long term.

Q3: Do you need artistic skill to try one of these designs?\
A3: Not necessarily. Stencils, painter's tape for geometric lines, and simpler designs like lettering or star bursts are all beginner-friendly starting points before attempting a full freehand piece like a nebula or portrait.

Q4: How long does a full pair of painted senior jeans take to complete?\
A4: Simpler designs can take a few hours, while detailed pieces with shading or lettering often take a full weekend across multiple painting and drying sessions. Rushing the drying time is the most common cause of smudged results.

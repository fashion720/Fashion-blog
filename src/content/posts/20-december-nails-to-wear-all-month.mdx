December is the one month when your nails can do the talking before you say a word, and these 20 December nails are made to last the whole month, not just one party. I have planned them the way I plan my own December: early-month looks that feel festive without shouting, mid-month party nails with real sparkle, Christmas-week designs, and a final look that carries you straight into New Year's Eve.

Each design below gives you the exact colours, the order to paint them, and the little fixes that stop chipping — because a December manicure has to survive wrapping paper, cold air, washing up, and three parties in a week. Pick one look for the whole month, or change it every week and work your way through the list.

Start with healthy nails and a good base coat, and the colour will do the rest. If you are still choosing what to wear with them, our [17 cosy Christmas sweater outfits to wear all December](https://outfitedits.com/posts/17-cozy-christmas-sweater-outfits-for-women-to-wear-all-december/) pair beautifully with almost every design here.

## 1. The Classic December Red

A true blue-red is the December manicure I reach for first, because it works on day one and still looks right on Christmas morning. Start by shaping your nails into a soft oval or short square, then push back your cuticles and wipe each nail with polish remover to take off any oil. Paint two thin coats of a classic red such as OPI Big Apple Red or Essie Forever Yummy, letting the first coat dry for two minutes before the second.

Thin coats are the whole secret here — one thick coat bubbles and peels by day three, while two thin ones dry smooth and last. Finish with a glossy top coat, and cap the free edge by running the brush lightly across the tip of each nail. That sealed edge is what stops the red chipping when you are typing, cooking, or unwrapping gifts.

Formula: shaped oval nails + two thin coats of classic blue-red polish + glossy top coat with a sealed free edge.

Tip: keep a bottle of the same red in your bag for tiny touch-ups, because a red tip chip shows instantly and a thirty-second dab on day five buys you another week.

## 2. Deep Burgundy with Gold French Tips

Burgundy feels richer than red once the evenings get dark, and a thin gold tip lifts it from everyday to party-ready without any nail art skill. Paint two coats of a deep burgundy such as Essie Bordeaux or OPI Malaga Wine and let them dry completely — at least ten minutes — before you touch the tips. Then, with a gold metallic polish and a steady hand, paint a thin curved line across the tip of each nail.

If freehand tips make you nervous, use French manicure guides or a strip of tape placed just below the tip as a stencil. Peel the guide off while the gold is still slightly wet for a clean edge, then seal everything with top coat. The burgundy base hides regrowth and small chips far better than a pale colour, so this is a brilliant choice for a manicure you want to stretch to ten days.

Formula: deep burgundy base + thin gold metallic French tip + glossy top coat.

Tip: paint the gold tip in one smooth sweep from one side to the other rather than dabbing, because dabbing leaves a wobbly edge that no top coat can hide.

![Deep burgundy nails with thin gold French tips on a winter evening background](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/2.webp)

## 3. Evergreen with Gold Foil

Evergreen is December's quiet luxury colour — it reads festive without a single snowflake in sight, and gold foil makes it look like you spent an hour in a salon. Start with two coats of a deep green such as Essie Off Tropic or OPI Stay Off the Lawn, and let the second coat dry until it is just tacky, not wet. Tear a small piece of gold foil, press it onto the tacky polish with tweezers, and lift it away to leave an irregular patch of gold.

Do not try to place the foil perfectly; the beauty of this look is that every nail is slightly different, like torn wrapping paper. Press foil onto one or two nails per hand, or a small corner on every nail if you want more sparkle. A thick top coat smooths the foil edges down so they do not catch on your clothes or peel off in the shower.

Formula: deep evergreen base + torn gold foil pressed onto tacky polish + thick smoothing top coat.

Tip: apply foil while the polish is tacky, not fully dry — if it is too wet the foil sinks and disappears, and if it is fully dry it will not stick at all.

![Deep evergreen nails with irregular gold foil accents on a cosy December background](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/3.webp)

## 4. Cosy Red Tartan Plaid

Tartan is the pattern of December jumpers, scarves, and wrapping paper, and it looks just as good on nails. Paint your nails with two coats of a warm red, then wait until they are completely dry. With a thin striping brush, paint two vertical and two horizontal lines in cream across each nail, then add one thin gold or black line beside each cream line to finish the woven plaid effect.

Keep the lines slightly uneven on purpose — real tartan has texture, and perfect geometry reads more like graph paper. A dotting tool helps if your hand shakes: use the side of the brush rather than the tip for straighter lines. This design takes patience, so do one hand at a time and let the first hand dry while you work on the second. Pair it with a plaid coat or scarf and the whole outfit suddenly looks planned.

Formula: warm red base + cream striping-brush crosshatch + thin gold accent lines + glossy top coat.

Tip: practise the plaid once on a piece of paper before your nails, because the spacing is easier to judge flat, and you will use far less polish remover fixing mistakes.

![Red tartan plaid nail art with cream and gold crosshatch lines on short nails](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/4.webp)

## 5. Snowflake Accent on Sheer Nude

If you love Christmas but hate busy nails, this is your December look: sheer nude on nine nails and one delicate snowflake per hand. Paint two coats of a sheer pink-nude such as Essie Ballet Slippers or OPI Bubble Bath, which lets your natural nail show through and makes regrowth invisible for two weeks. On the ring finger of each hand, use a white polish and a fine dotting tool to draw the snowflake.

Draw a small cross, then an X over it, then add tiny dots at the end of each arm — six arms, six dots, and you have a snowflake. It does not need to be symmetrical to read as a snowflake at arm's length. A matte top coat on the nude nails and a glossy top coat just on the snowflake nail gives a subtle contrast that people notice up close without the whole manicure shouting.

Formula: sheer nude base on all nails + white snowflake drawn with a dotting tool on each ring finger + glossy top coat.

Tip: draw the snowflake while the nude base is fully dry, and keep your hand resting on a table so it does not shake — a wobbly snowflake still looks charming, but a smudged one does not.

![Sheer nude nails with a delicate white snowflake accent on the ring finger](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/5.webp)

## 6. Gold Glitter Ombre

A gold glitter ombre is December's easiest party nail, because glitter hides every mistake and the fade means regrowth barely shows. Paint two coats of a warm nude or soft gold base, then take a gold glitter polish and a makeup sponge. Dab the glitter onto the sponge, then press it onto the tip of each nail, building it up in layers — heavier at the tip, fading as you move down toward the cuticle.

Three light sponge layers beat one heavy brush coat, which goes on patchy and thick. Clean up the skin around your nails with a small brush dipped in remover while the glitter is still slightly damp, then seal with two coats of top coat — glitter is rough without it, and the second coat is what makes it feel smooth and look like glass. Strong nails help glitter last longer, and [Healthline's guide to strengthening nails](https://www.healthline.com/health/how-to-strengthen-nails) explains the daily habits that stop peeling and breakage before you paint.

Formula: nude or gold base + gold glitter sponged from tip to middle + two coats of top coat.

Tip: peel-off base coat under glitter saves your nails at removal time, because glitter is the hardest polish to take off and scrubbing damages the nail underneath.

![Gold glitter ombre nails fading from the tip down to a nude base](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/6.webp)

## 7. Candy Cane Stripes

Candy cane stripes are pure December joy, and they are far easier than they look if you work in the right order. Paint two coats of white polish and let them dry completely — this is not a step to rush, because red over wet white smears into pink. Then, with a thin striping brush and a bright red, paint diagonal lines across each nail, keeping the spacing even and the lines the same width.

Alternate the direction on each hand if you like, or keep them all the same for a cleaner look. If a line goes wrong, wait for it to dry and paint a white line over it rather than wiping the whole nail — wiping takes the white base with it. One accent nail per hand is enough if full stripes feel too much, and the look pairs perfectly with a red lip and a cream jumper.

Formula: white base + red diagonal stripes painted with a striping brush + glossy top coat.

Tip: load the striping brush lightly and wipe the excess on the bottle neck, because a overloaded brush floods the nail and the stripe spreads wider than you planned.

![White nails with red diagonal candy cane stripes on a festive December background](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/7.webp)

## 8. Emerald Velvet Cat-Eye

Velvet cat-eye nails use a magnetic polish to create a soft shimmer that moves when you tilt your hand, and emerald is the December shade that shows it best. Paint one coat of the magnetic emerald polish, then hold the magnet that comes with the bottle about five millimetres above the wet nail for ten seconds, without touching the polish. The magnet pulls the metallic particles into a glowing line or a soft halo, depending on the magnet shape.

Work one nail at a time — if you paint all ten first, the first nails will be dry before the magnet can move anything. Do a second coat the same way if you want a deeper colour, then seal with top coat. The effect looks expensive in low winter light and candlelight, which is exactly when December parties happen, and it needs no stickers, gems, or extra tools beyond the magnet in the box.

Formula: magnetic emerald polish + magnet held above each wet nail for ten seconds + glossy top coat.

Tip: keep the magnet still while it works — moving it mid-count drags the shimmer into a blur, and you cannot fix a blurred cat-eye without starting that nail again.

![Emerald velvet cat-eye nails with a magnetic shimmer line in candlelight](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/8.webp)

## 9. Frosted White French Tips

A frosted French is the December version of the classic manicure: the same clean white tip, but with a soft shimmer that looks like frost on a window. Paint a sheer pink or milky white base over the whole nail, then paint the tip with a white polish that has a fine silver shimmer in it, following the natural smile line of your nail. A thin brush gives you more control than the wide bottle brush, especially on short nails.

If your tips are uneven, a cleanup brush dipped in remover tidies the line in seconds. This look is made for short nails — it makes them look longer and neater without extensions — and it goes with every December outfit, from a work blazer to a sequin dress. For the outfit side of that range, our [17 stylish Christmas party outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/17-stylish-christmas-party-outfits-for-women/) show how one French manicure can carry you through every event on your calendar.

Formula: sheer pink base + shimmery white French tip painted with a thin brush + glossy top coat.

Tip: paint the tip slightly thicker in the centre and thinner at the sides, because that curve follows your nail's natural shape and makes short nails look longer.

![Frosted white French tip nails with a subtle silver shimmer on short nails](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/9.webp)

## 10. Cranberry Chrome

Cranberry chrome is the grown-up glitter: a deep berry base with a mirror-like shine that catches light without a single speck of glitter. Paint two coats of a dark cranberry or burgundy, then apply a chrome powder over a no-wipe top coat while it is still slightly tacky, rubbing it in with a silicone applicator or a fingertip covered in a plastic glove. Buff it until the surface turns smooth and reflective, then seal it with two coats of top coat.

Chrome powder is messy, so do it over a tray or a sheet of paper and wipe the skin around your nails before the final top coat. The result lasts best on gel, but regular polish works too if you let every layer dry properly and avoid hot water for the first few hours. This is the colour I would pick for a December wedding or a formal dinner, because it reads as jewellery on your hands.

Formula: dark cranberry base + chrome powder buffed over no-wipe top coat + two sealing top coats.

Tip: use a no-wipe top coat under the powder, not a regular one — regular top coat stays tacky in the wrong way and the powder clumps instead of buffing smooth.

![Cranberry chrome nails with a smooth mirror-like berry shine](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/10.webp)

## 11. The Christmas Tree Accent Nail

One Christmas tree nail per hand is the sweet spot: festive, fun, and still wearable at work on Monday. Paint the other nails a deep green or a classic red, then paint the ring finger a contrasting colour — cream or gold works best. On that accent nail, use a green polish and a thin brush to draw a small triangle, then add a second, slightly wider triangle below it, and a tiny brown trunk at the bottom.

Add three tiny dots in red, gold, and silver as baubles, and a small gold star or a dot of gold glitter at the top. Keep the tree small and centred — a tree that fills the whole nail looks crowded, while a small one reads as a deliberate design. This is the nail children notice first, and it photographs beautifully holding a mug of hot chocolate or a wrapped gift.

Formula: red or green base on nine nails + cream accent nail with a two-triangle green tree, bauble dots and gold star + top coat.

Tip: draw the tree with the brush barely loaded, because a thick triangle bleeds at the edges, while two thin passes give you a crisp shape you can tidy easily.

![Christmas tree accent nail with green tree and gold star on a cream base](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/11.webp)

## 12. Winter White with Silver Glitter

Winter white nails are December's cleanest look, and a dusting of silver glitter stops them looking plain. Paint two coats of a soft white or warm ivory — a pure bright white can look harsh on very pale skin, so an ivory or a milky white is usually more flattering. Then, using a sponge, dab fine silver glitter just onto the tips, or paint one full glitter accent nail per hand.

White shows every ridge and stain, so a ridge-filling base coat matters here more than with any dark colour. Buff the nail surface gently before you start, and wipe with remover to take off any old polish stains. This manicure looks stunning with silver jewellery and a white or cream knit, and it is the best choice if you want December nails that still look right in a January meeting.

Formula: ridge-filling base coat + two coats of milky white + silver glitter sponged onto the tips + glossy top coat.

Tip: wear gloves for washing up and cleaning, because white polish stains from turmeric, hair dye, and even dark denim, and one stain ruins the whole clean effect.

![Winter white nails with fine silver glitter dusted on the tips](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/12.webp)

## 13. Holly Berry Red with Green Leaves

Holly is December's most elegant botanical, and it is surprisingly simple to paint. Start with a deep green or a sheer nude base — nude makes the holly pop, green makes it feel like a wreath. On the ring finger, use a dotting tool to place three small red dots close together for the berries, then use a thin brush to paint two small pointed leaves in a brighter green beside them.

Add a thin white line down the centre of each leaf for the vein, and the design is done. Keep the berries small; oversized dots look like cherries rather than holly. This design works beautifully on short nails because the art sits on one nail and the rest of the manicure stays simple. It is also the design I would choose for a family Christmas lunch, where you want something pretty that does not distract from the day.

Formula: nude or deep green base + three red berry dots + two pointed green leaves with white veins on each ring finger + top coat.

Tip: place the berries first and the leaves second, because the leaves should tuck behind the berries like real holly, and painting them first makes the berries look pasted on top.

![Holly berry nail art with red berries and green leaves on a nude base](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/13.webp)

## 14. Midnight Navy with Gold Stars

Midnight navy with gold stars is December night sky on your nails, and it works from the first week of the month right through to New Year's Eve. Paint two coats of a deep navy such as Essie Midnight Cami or OPI Russian Navy, and let them dry fully. Then use a gold metallic polish and a dotting tool to place tiny gold dots and four-pointed stars across the nails — a few per nail, not a crowded sky.

Draw a star by crossing two short lines and adding a dot in the centre, or keep it to dots alone if freehand stars feel too fiddly. A matte top coat makes the navy look like velvet and the gold look like real stars, while a glossy top coat makes the whole thing read more like a party. Either works; I prefer matte for daytime and glossy for evenings out. Pair this with a navy coat or a silver dress and the whole look feels intentional.

Formula: deep navy base + gold dots and four-pointed stars placed with a dotting tool + matte or glossy top coat.

Tip: place the largest star near the cuticle and smaller dots toward the tip, because that spacing looks like a real night sky and stops the design feeling random.

![Midnight navy nails with tiny gold stars and dots like a night sky](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/14.webp)

## 15. Gingerbread Brown with Icing Details

Gingerbread nails smell like December even though they are only polish, and the icing detail is what makes people smile. Paint two coats of a warm milk-chocolate brown, then use a white polish and a thin brush to draw wavy icing lines across the tips, tiny dots down the centre, and a small swirl on the accent nail. Think of decorating a real biscuit: the lines do not need to be perfect, because real icing is never perfect either.

Add a tiny red dot as a button on the accent nail if you want the full gingerbread-man effect. This warm brown is flattering on every skin tone, and it hides chips better than any pale colour, so it is a practical choice for the busiest week of December. It also pairs with camel coats, brown boots, and cream knits in a way that red and green never quite manage.

Formula: warm chocolate brown base + white icing waves, dots and swirls painted with a thin brush + glossy top coat.

Tip: let the brown dry for a full ten minutes before the white icing, because white over tacky brown turns pinkish-brown and the whole biscuit effect is lost.

![Gingerbread brown nails with white icing swirls and dots like decorated biscuits](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/15.webp)

## 16. Silver Glitter Party Nails

By mid-December the parties start, and silver glitter is the fastest way to look dressed up without a dress change. Paint a base of silver glitter polish in two coats, or paint a nude base and layer a chunky silver glitter over it for more texture. Chunky glitter — the kind with different-sized pieces — catches light better than fine glitter and hides the nail underneath, so one generous coat can be enough.

The trick with full glitter is removal, so use a peel-off base coat or soak cotton pads in remover and wrap each nail in foil for ten minutes rather than scrubbing. Glitter also needs two top coats to feel smooth; one leaves a rough surface that catches on tights and knitwear. Wear this with a black dress or a silver top, and your hands become part of the outfit rather than an afterthought. For the dress itself, our [15 glamorous holiday dress outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/15-glamorous-holiday-dress-outfits-for-women/) are the perfect match for full silver glitter.

Formula: peel-off base coat + two coats of chunky silver glitter + two smoothing top coats.

Tip: press the glitter polish on rather than brushing it in long strokes, because brushing drags the glitter pieces off the nail and leaves bald patches.

## 17. Forest Green and Cream Cable-Knit Nails

Cable-knit nails — also called sweater nails — recreate the texture of a chunky jumper on your nails, and they are made for December. Paint two coats of a forest green or cream polish and cure or dry them fully. Then, using the same colour in a slightly thicker layer, paint two vertical lines down the centre of each nail and a small zigzag or braid shape between them, using a thin brush or a dotting tool dragged slowly.

Dust the wet design with clear acrylic powder or a matte top coat while it is still tacky to give it a soft, raised, knitted texture. The effect is subtle at arm's length and delightful up close, which is exactly what a cosy December look should be. Keep the other nails plain in the same colour so the knit design stands out, and pair it with a real chunky knit for the full effect.

Formula: forest green or cream base + raised cable-knit lines painted in the same colour + matte top coat or clear powder dusted on top.

Tip: work one nail at a time and dust the powder on while the design is still wet, because once it dries the powder will not stick and the knit texture disappears.

## 18. Ruby Red with Gold Confetti

Ruby red with gold confetti is Christmas morning in a bottle: a deep jewel red scattered with tiny gold pieces that catch the light every time you move. Paint two coats of a ruby red such as OPI I'm Not Really a Waitress, then paint one coat of a gold confetti topper — the kind with small hexagons, stars, and dots in one bottle — over the top. One coat gives a scattered effect; two coats give a full party.

The confetti topper does the decorating for you, so this is the best December design for anyone who does not trust their freehand skills. It also layers beautifully over a burgundy or a cranberry base if ruby feels too bright. A glossy top coat is essential here, because confetti pieces sit slightly raised and need sealing down, or they will catch and lift within a day.

Formula: ruby red base + one coat of gold confetti topper + glossy sealing top coat.

Tip: dab the confetti topper on with the brush rather than sweeping it, because sweeping pushes all the pieces to one side and leaves the other side bare.

![Ruby red nails with scattered gold confetti glitter pieces](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/18.webp)

## 19. Icy Blue with Snow Glitter

Icy blue is the December colour for anyone who finds red and green too traditional, and it looks like fresh snow in sunlight. Paint two coats of a pale icy blue, then sponge fine white and silver glitter onto the tips to create a frosted edge, fading it down toward the middle of the nail. A pale blue base shows streaks easily, so use thin coats and let each one dry fully before the next — rushing is what makes pale polish look patchy.

This is a lovely choice for Christmas week if your outfit is silver, white, or pale grey, and it photographs beautifully outdoors in winter light. [Nail polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nail_polish) comes in far more finishes than most people realise — creme, shimmer, glitter, and metallic all behave differently on the nail — and a pale shimmer blue is far more forgiving than a flat creme blue, so pick a shimmer if your nails are ridged.

Formula: pale icy blue shimmer base + white and silver glitter sponged onto the tips + glossy top coat.

Tip: use a white base coat under very pale blue, because pale blue over a yellow-toned natural nail turns greenish, while white underneath keeps it true icy blue.

![Icy blue nails with white and silver snow glitter fading from the tips](/images/content/20-december-nails-to-wear-all-month/19.webp)

## 20. New Year's Eve Countdown Gold-to-Black

This final look is your bridge from December into January: black at the cuticle fading into gold glitter at the tip, like a countdown clock striking midnight. Paint two coats of black polish, then sponge gold glitter from the tip upward, building it heavier at the tip and fading it out by the middle of the nail. A second sponge layer on just the very tip gives the fade a real gradient rather than a hard line.

Black and gold is the most forgiving combination for a New Year's Eve party — chips barely show on black, and gold glitter hides everything else. It also means you do not need a fresh manicure on 1 January; this one still looks intentional a week later. Wear it with gold jewellery and a black dress, and you are ready for midnight. Formula: black base + gold glitter sponged from the tip fading toward the middle + two glossy top coats.

Tip: clean the gold glitter off your cuticles with a flat brush dipped in remover before the top coat, because glitter on skin looks messy and it is much harder to remove once sealed.

### Your December Nails Checklist

Before your December manicure, run through this list so it actually lasts all month:

- **Base coat every time** — it stops red, green, and burgundy staining your nails yellow.
- **Two thin coats, never one thick one** — thin coats dry smooth and chip far less.
- **Cap the free edge** — run polish and top coat across the tip to seal it against chipping.
- **Top coat every two to three days** — a fresh top coat revives shine and adds a new protective layer.
- **Cuticle oil nightly** — December cold dries cuticles fast, and oiled cuticles stop polish lifting at the edges.
- **Gloves for cleaning and washing up** — hot water and detergent are the fastest way to kill a manicure.
- **Keep the polish for touch-ups** — a thirty-second dab on day five buys you another week of wear.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What December nail colour lasts the longest?

Deep reds, burgundies, and dark greens last longest because chips and regrowth show less on dark colours than on pale ones. A dark base with a glossy top coat, refreshed every two to three days, can easily last ten days to two weeks. Pale white and nude shades show chips faster, so save those for the week of the party rather than the start of the month.

### How do I stop my December manicure chipping?

Use a base coat, paint two thin coats instead of one thick one, cap the free edge of each nail with polish and top coat, and reapply top coat every two to three days. Wear gloves for washing up and cleaning, and use cuticle oil nightly — dry cuticles lift polish at the edges. Avoid hot showers and baths in the first few hours after painting, when polish is still hardening.

### Can I wear Christmas nail art to work?

Yes, if you keep it to one accent nail per hand or choose a subtle design such as a snowflake on nude, a gold French tip, or a single Christmas tree nail. A full set of candy cane stripes or glitter on every nail reads as party nails, while one accent nail reads as festive and polished. When in doubt, pick the design you would feel comfortable wearing in a meeting.

### How do I remove glitter polish without damaging my nails?

Soak a cotton pad in acetone remover, place it on the nail, and wrap the fingertip in foil for ten minutes, then slide the pad off — the glitter should come with it. Do not scrub or peel glitter off, because that pulls up layers of your natural nail. A peel-off base coat under glitter makes removal far easier next time.

### What nails should I wear for Christmas week versus New Year's Eve?

Christmas week suits red, green, plaid, snowflakes, and Christmas tree designs, while New Year's Eve suits black, gold, silver, chrome, and glitter — the countdown look in section 20 bridges both. If you only want one manicure for the whole festive week, pick a deep red or burgundy with gold, which reads as Christmas on the 25th and as party-ready on the 31st.

Velvet is the fabric winter was made for, and these 20 luxe velvet outfits show you how to wear it without looking like you raided a costume box. Each look below gives you the exact pieces, the order to layer them, and a simple formula you can copy with what you already own.

You will learn which velvet colours flatter you, how to balance shine with matte fabrics, and how to take one velvet piece from a family dinner to a New Year's Eve party. Every outfit includes a formula, a fit tip, and a shoe pairing that works on cold pavements.

Start with the velvet you own, or use the buying checklist at the end before you shop. By the last look you will have a full winter rotation — and for even more evening combinations, our [17 stylish Christmas party outfits](/posts/17-stylish-christmas-party-outfits-for-women/) pair perfectly with these velvet looks. If you want a second opinion on making velvet feel modern rather than old-fashioned, [Who What Wear's guide to wearing velvet](https://www.whowhatwear.com/how-to-wear-velvet) shows how editors style it now.

## 1. The Emerald Velvet Midi Dress

An emerald velvet midi dress is the easiest party outfit you will ever wear, because the fabric does all the talking. Pick a long-sleeve style with a defined waist and a hem that falls just below the knee, so it moves well on a dance floor and still looks neat sitting down at dinner. The deep green reads festive without a single sequin.

Black strappy heels and a small black clutch keep the focus on the dress, and one pair of gold drop earrings is plenty. Skip a necklace — the high neckline and the velvet pile already fill the space near your face. This suits almost every skin tone, and it photographs beautifully in warm party lighting.

Formula: emerald velvet midi dress + black strappy heels + small black clutch + gold drop earrings.

Tip: velvet shows pressure marks, so hang the dress instead of folding it, and steam it from the inside to lift any creases without flattening the pile.

## 2. The Black Velvet Blazer with Straight Jeans

A black velvet blazer over a white silk camisole and dark straight jeans is velvet at its most wearable. The blazer gives you the richness of the fabric, while the jeans keep it grounded enough for a drinks party or a smart dinner out. Choose a blazer with a single button and a nipped waist so it shapes rather than boxes you.

Pointed-toe black heels and a slim leather bag finish it, and a thin gold chain is the only jewellery you need. This is the outfit for the woman who owns one velvet piece and wants it to earn its keep beyond December.

Formula: black velvet blazer + white silk camisole + dark straight jeans + black pointed-toe heels.

Tip: brush velvet in one direction with a clothes brush before you go out — strokes going different ways are what make velvet look patchy in photos.

![Woman wearing a black velvet blazer with a white camisole and dark straight jeans](/images/content/20-luxe-velvet-outfits-for-women-this-winter/2.webp)

## 3. The Burgundy Velvet Slip Dress

A burgundy velvet slip dress layered over a black fitted turtleneck solves the two problems slip dresses always have in winter: cold shoulders and too much skin at a family party. The turtleneck warms the look and makes the velvet read deliberate rather than evening-only. Keep the slip dress midi-length so the proportions stay elegant.

Opaque black tights and heeled ankle boots close the outfit, with a small structured bag in black. Burgundy flatters warm and cool skin tones alike, and the darker shade hides the odd mark far better than a pale velvet ever will.

Formula: burgundy velvet slip dress + black fitted turtleneck + opaque tights + black heeled ankle boots.

Tip: slip dresses cut on the bias stretch as you wear them, so buy your true size and let the turtleneck underneath take up any slack.

![Woman wearing a burgundy velvet slip dress layered over a black turtleneck](/images/content/20-luxe-velvet-outfits-for-women-this-winter/3.webp)

## 4. The Navy Velvet Wide-Leg Trousers

Navy velvet wide-leg trousers with a cream silk blouse are quietly the most expensive-looking combination in this guide. The trousers should skim the floor over a low heel, and the blouse should be tucked in with a slim belt so your waist stays visible under all that drape. Navy velvet catches light softly, so it reads rich rather than shiny.

Add pointed-toe nude heels and a cream clutch, and keep jewellery to pearl studs. This is a brilliant choice for taller women, and for anyone who wants a party outfit she can sit, eat and dance in without adjusting anything.

Formula: navy velvet wide-leg trousers + cream silk blouse + slim belt + nude pointed-toe heels.

Tip: hem velvet trousers while wearing the exact shoes you will pair them with — velvet pools and drags fast, and a hem that is even half an inch too long will fray within a night.

![Woman wearing navy velvet wide-leg trousers with a cream silk blouse](/images/content/20-luxe-velvet-outfits-for-women-this-winter/4.webp)

## 5. The Plum Velvet Wrap Dress

A plum velvet wrap dress flatters nearly every shape because you set the waist exactly where yours is. Look for a true wrap with a knee-length or midi hem and three-quarter sleeves, which cover the upper arm while keeping the neckline open enough for a pendant necklace. Plum is softer than black but just as slimming.

Wear it with block-heel pumps in black and a small top-handle bag. If you are between sizes, size down — wrap dresses in stretch velvet give a little, and a loose wrap gapes at the chest when you sit.

Formula: plum velvet wrap dress + black block-heel pumps + small top-handle bag + pendant necklace.

Tip: tie the wrap at the side rather than the front, so the knot does not add bulk at your stomach in photos.

![Woman wearing a plum velvet wrap dress with black block-heel pumps](/images/content/20-luxe-velvet-outfits-for-women-this-winter/5.webp)

## 6. The Forest Green Velvet Mini Dress

A forest green velvet mini dress with long sleeves is the party dress for women who want legs on show and everything else covered. The long sleeves balance the short hem, and the dark green keeps it from reading too young. Add sheer black tights in 40 denier so the outfit is genuinely warm on a December night.

Knee-high black boots turn this into a going-out look that survives a cold walk from the car, while a small crossbody bag keeps your hands free. Skip the mini if the party involves sitting on low sofas all evening — a midi will serve you better.

Formula: forest green velvet mini dress + sheer black tights + black knee-high boots + small crossbody bag.

Tip: test the dress by sitting down in it before the night — if the hem rides up more than an inch, wear a longer style or swap to opaque tights.

## 7. The Chocolate Velvet Co-ord Set

A chocolate velvet co-ord — a cropped jacket and matching wide-leg trousers — is the modern way to wear head-to-toe velvet without looking like you are in costume. The trick is the break in the middle: a cream or ivory top underneath splits the brown into two pieces and stops it reading as a single block. Chocolate is warmer and fresher than black this winter.

Gold heeled sandals and a tan leather bag warm the palette further, and a slicked-back low bun keeps the outfit sharp. Wear the two pieces separately later — the jacket over jeans, the trousers with a knit — and the set pays for itself.

Formula: chocolate velvet co-ord set + cream top + gold heeled sandals + tan leather bag.

Tip: buy co-ords in the same dye lot and wash them together, because velvet fades unevenly and a mismatched set is obvious in daylight.

## 8. The Black Velvet Jumpsuit

A black velvet jumpsuit is the one-piece answer to every 'what do I wear' panic before a party. Choose a wide-leg style with a V-neck and a defined waist, and the jumpsuit does the work of a dress with the comfort of trousers. The V-neck is the place for one statement — a crystal pendant or a bold lip, not both.

Block-heel sandals keep you steady on dance floors, and a metallic clutch adds the only shine the outfit needs. Petite women should look for a jumpsuit with a shorter torso measurement, or the waist will sit too low and shorten the legs.

Formula: black velvet jumpsuit + block-heel sandals + metallic clutch.

Tip: always try a jumpsuit sitting down — a rise that feels fine standing can pull badly at the shoulders when you sit, and velvet shows every pull line.

## 9. The Wine Velvet A-Line Skirt

A wine velvet A-line skirt with a black fitted knit is the outfit for a Christmas dinner where you want to look festive sitting down. The A-line shape skims the hip and flares just enough to move gracefully, and the matte knit next to the velvet stops the shine from taking over. A midi length keeps it elegant.

Ballet flats in black keep it comfortable through a long evening, or swap to a low block heel if you want height. A thin black belt marks the waist where the knit meets the skirt, and small gold hoops finish it.

Formula: wine velvet A-line midi skirt + black fitted knit + thin black belt + black ballet flats.

Tip: A-line velvet skirts crease at the seat, so stand and smooth the back with your palm before photos, and carry a small lint roller for the knit.

## 10. The Teal Velvet Off-Shoulder Top

A teal velvet off-shoulder top with black tailored trousers is a colour decision that pays off. Teal is rare enough to turn heads, and the off-shoulder line shows just enough skin to feel like a party without a low neckline. The trousers should be high-waisted and straight-legged so the proportions stay long.

Black pointed-toe heels and a black clutch ground the colour, and a pair of drop earrings echoes the neckline. Keep your hair up or swept to one side so the shoulder line stays visible — hair hiding the detail defeats the point of the top.

Formula: teal velvet off-shoulder top + black tailored trousers + black pointed-toe heels + drop earrings.

Tip: off-shoulder tops slip, so use fashion tape at the top edge and choose a style with a silicone grip strip inside if you plan to dance.

## 11. The Cream Velvet Midi Dress

A cream velvet midi dress is the bravest choice on this list and the most rewarding in photos. The pale pile glows in candlelight, and a simple long-sleeve, high-neck cut keeps it elegant rather than bridal. Wear it with the confidence that cream velvet demands: clean lines, no competing colours.

Nude pointed-toe heels and a small gold clutch are all it needs, plus a soft pink lip. Fair warning — cream shows every mark, so this is a one-evening dress for indoor parties, not for a night that starts with a long walk in the rain.

Formula: cream velvet midi dress + nude pointed-toe heels + small gold clutch.

Tip: spray cream velvet lightly with a fabric protector before the first wear, and blot — never rub — any mark, since rubbing crushes the pile permanently.

Opaque tights and black knee-high boots make it winter-proof, and a small chain bag adds a little shine. This is a strong choice for women who prefer structure to drape, and it layers beautifully under a long wool coat on the way there.

Formula: charcoal velvet blazer dress + opaque tights + black knee-high boots + small chain bag.

Tip: a blazer dress must close without pulling at the button — if it only fits standing still, go up a size and have the waist taken in.

A rust velvet slip skirt with an ivory chunky knit is the high-low pairing that makes velvet feel modern. The knit softens the shine, the rust warms the ivory, and the slip skirt's bias cut moves beautifully when you walk. Tuck the front of the knit in so the waistline reads clearly.

Tan ankle boots and a woven tote keep it daytime-friendly, which makes this the rare velvet outfit that works for a lunch party or a family gathering, not just an evening event. Rust suits warm skin tones best, and it pairs with gold jewellery far better than silver.

Formula: rust velvet slip skirt + ivory chunky knit + tan ankle boots + woven tote.

Tip: bias-cut skirts grow longer as the day goes on, so check the hem after an hour of wear and choose a length that allows for a little drop.

## 14. The Midnight Blue Velvet Gown

A midnight blue velvet gown is for the one formal night of the season — the ball, the gala, the black-tie dinner. Midnight blue photographs richer than black under evening lights.

Silver strappy heels and a small silver clutch are the right metals here, and hair swept up shows the neckline. Keep the gown's train minimal or none — a long train on velvet picks up everything on the floor.

### Your Velvet Buying and Care Checklist

Before you buy, or before you wear the piece you already own, run through this list:

- **One velvet piece at a time** — in daylight, two velvet pieces only work as a matched set or in the evening.
- **Matte fabrics next to it** — denim, knits, cotton and plain leather stop the shine from taking over.
- **A defined waist** — a belt, a wrap tie or a tucked-in top keeps velvet from reading shapeless.
- **The right weight** — lined stretch velvet for winter warmth, lighter cotton velvet for indoor parties.
- **Steam, never iron** — an iron flattens the pile permanently, while steam lifts creases safely.
- **Hang, don't fold** — folding leaves pressure marks in the pile that are hard to remove.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What colour velvet should I buy first?

Buy black if you want one piece that works everywhere, burgundy if you want festive without trying, or emerald if you want a colour that photographs beautifully and suits most skin tones. Black hides marks and layers easily, burgundy pairs with gold jewellery, and emerald works with both black and cream. Pick a shade you already own shoes and bags in, so the velvet joins your wardrobe instead of starting a new one. For more polished all-neutral evening looks, see our [20 classy elegant winter outfits](/posts/20-classy-elegant-winter-outfits-to-pin-and-wear/).

### Can I wear velvet during the day?

Yes — pair one velvet piece with matte, everyday fabrics like denim, a chunky knit, or a cotton shirt. A velvet blazer over jeans, a velvet skirt with a sweater, or velvet trousers with a simple blouse all read daytime-appropriate. The rule is one velvet piece at a time in daylight, and darker colours like navy or burgundy work better than bright red or gold before dark. [Who What Wear's second velvet guide](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/winter/how-to-wear-velvet) shows three more non-holiday ways to wear the fabric.

### How do I stop velvet looking crushed or shiny in photos?

Brush the pile in one direction with a clothes brush before you go out, and steam from the inside rather than ironing, which flattens velvet permanently. Avoid sitting on rough surfaces, and smooth the seat of skirts and trousers with your palm when you stand. Matte fabrics next to the velvet — a cotton shirt, a knit, plain leather — also stop the shine from taking over.

### Is velvet warm enough for winter?

Velvet is a medium-weight fabric, so it insulates better than silk or satin but it is not a coat on its own. Layer it over a fitted turtleneck, add opaque tights under dresses and skirts, and wear a long wool coat over the top outdoors. Stretch velvet with a lining is the warmest choice, while unlined cotton velvet is better for indoor parties. For more cold-weather layering, see our [16 classic wool coat outfits](/posts/16-classic-wool-coat-outfits-for-women/).

### How do I care for velvet at home?

Check the label first — silk velvet is dry-clean only, while cotton and polyester velvet can usually be hand-washed cold or machine-washed on a gentle cycle inside out. Never iron velvet directly; steam it instead. Hang velvet pieces rather than folding them, keep them out of direct sunlight, and brush the pile after wear to keep it looking even.

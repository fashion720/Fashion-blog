Christmas family photos only happen once a year, so the outfits in them deserve a real plan — not a last-minute scramble through everyone's closets. The secret to photos that look polished instead of costume-y is coordination, not identical outfits: pick one colour palette, repeat it in different textures and silhouettes, and let every person wear a version that suits them.

This guide gives you 15 complete, photo-ready looks for women — each one with step-by-step styling, an exact outfit formula, and practical tips for matching the rest of the family without looking like a uniform. Whether your shoot is by the tree, in the snow, or dressed up for the evening, you will find a combination that photographs beautifully and feels comfortable enough to actually enjoy the day.

## 1. Classic Red and Green Matching Sets

Nothing says Christmas in a photo like classic red and green — but the trick is keeping the two colours from fighting each other. Choose one dominant colour per person and let the other appear as an accent, so the group reads as coordinated rather than a patchwork quilt.

Start with a deep red knit dress or a red blouse with dark green trousers as your base. Add a forest-green cardigan or a plaid scarf that picks up both colours. Keep shoes simple: brown leather ankle boots or black ballet flats ground the look. For the rest of the family, flip the ratio — green knits for the kids, red accessories for your partner.

Formula: Red knit dress + forest-green cardigan + brown ankle boots + gold stud earrings.

Tip: avoid bright, candy-apple red and neon green together; deep, muted tones photograph far more richly. Test your reds in daylight before the shoot, not after.

## 2. Coordinated Burgundy and Cream

Burgundy and cream is the most forgiving palette for family photos: the warm wine tone flatters every skin tone, and cream softens the whole frame.

Begin with a burgundy midi dress in a soft fabric like crepe or velvet. Layer a cream chunky-knit cardigan over your shoulders for texture and warmth. Add cream or nude tights and burgundy suede boots. For the family, dress the kids in cream knits with burgundy bows or scarves, and give your partner a burgundy sweater over a cream shirt.

Formula: Burgundy midi dress + cream chunky cardigan + nude tights + burgundy suede boots.

Tip: keep all the creams in the same family — warm ivory with warm ivory, not a mix of stark white and yellowed cream, or the mismatch will show in photos. ## 3. Matching Plaid Family Looks

Plaid is the unofficial uniform of Christmas family photos, and it works because the pattern itself does the coordinating. The rule is simple: one plaid pattern per family, repeated in different pieces.

Pick one family pattern: a classic red-and-black buffalo check or a green-and-navy tartan. Wear it as a plaid midi skirt with a solid black turtleneck, or a plaid shacket over a plain dress. Repeat the same plaid in smaller doses — a scarf for your partner, a dress for your daughter, a bow tie for your son. Keep everything else solid and neutral so the pattern stays the hero.

Formula: Plaid midi skirt + black turtleneck + black tights + tan leather boots + matching plaid scarf.

Tip: buy the plaid pieces from the same collection if you can; two "red plaids" from different brands rarely match, and the difference shows in photos. Who What Wear has a useful roundup of [holiday outfit ideas](https://www.whowhatwear.com/holiday-outfits) if you want more pattern inspiration.

## 4. Elegant Black and Gold

Black and gold turns a family photo into a formal portrait — perfect if your shoot is in the evening or in front of a dressed-up tree. Black keeps everyone slim and unified, while gold adds the festive sparkle.

Start with a black midi dress with a little texture: velvet, pleated crepe, or a subtle sequin bodice. Add gold in measured doses: a thin gold belt, gold hoops, and metallic gold heels or flats. For the family, black outfits with gold accessories work for everyone: a gold bow tie, a gold headband, gold shoes for the little ones.

Formula: Black velvet midi dress + gold belt + gold hoop earrings + metallic gold heels.

Tip: gold photographs best in warm indoor light, so schedule this look for golden hour or a well-lit living room. Avoid silver jewellery here — mixing metals in one photo looks accidental.

## 5. Cozy Knit Matching Sweaters

Matching knit sweaters are the cosiest option on this list and the easiest to pull off, because comfort is built in. They suit morning photos by the tree, cocoa in hand, when nobody wants a formal dress.

Choose a chunky knit in a shared colour — cream, cranberry, or forest green — and let each person wear their own style of sweater in that colour. Yours could be an oversized turtleneck sweater dress; the kids get crewnecks. Add dark jeans or leggings, warm socks, and slippers or boots. Matching touches — the same beanie or scarf — tie the group together.

Formula: Oversized cream turtleneck sweater dress + dark leggings + brown boots + matching knit beanie.

Tip: knits can look bulky on camera, so define your waist with a thin belt or half-tuck, and keep the sweater colour the same across the family, not "close enough" shades. ## 6. Winter White Family Looks

An all-white or winter-white palette makes family photos look bright, clean, and almost editorial — especially against a green tree or a snowy background. Mix textures so white-on-white does not flatten into one pale blur.

Start with a white or ivory sweater dress, then layer a cream faux-fur vest or a white wool coat over it. Add white or nude boots and a soft scarf. Give each person a different white texture: cable knit, faux fur, or crisp cotton. One accent — a red ribbon, holly, or a burgundy lip — stops the look washing out.

Formula: Ivory sweater dress + cream faux-fur vest + white wool coat + nude boots + red lip.

Tip: winter white needs good light, so shoot near a window or outdoors before noon. Keep a lint roller handy — white shows every pet hair and speck of dust, and the camera will find them all.

## 7. Velvet Dresses for Mum and Daughter

Velvet is made for Christmas photos: it catches light softly, looks rich in every colour, and instantly makes a mother-daughter match feel special without being identical. Choose this look for one standout mantelpiece frame.

Pick matching velvet dresses in the same colour — emerald, burgundy, or navy — but in different cuts: a fit-and-flare midi for you, an A-line dress for your daughter. Add black tights and Mary Jane shoes for her, and heeled ankle boots for you. Keep jewellery minimal — small pearl or gold studs let the fabric do the talking.

Formula: Emerald velvet fit-and-flare dress + black tights + heeled ankle boots + pearl stud earrings.

Tip: velvet shows pressure marks, so hang dresses, never fold them, and steam — never iron — before the shoot. Sit carefully between shots so the pile does not crush. ## 8. Rustic Flannel and Denim

Rustic flannel and denim is the relaxed, outdoorsy choice — ideal for farmhouse settings, porch photos, or a shoot beside a woodpile and fairy lights.

Start with a red or green flannel shirt, worn open over a white tee or tucked into high-waisted jeans. Add a denim jacket and brown leather boots. Repeat the same flannel in shirts, dresses, or scarves, and keep denim consistent — all dark wash or all light wash, not a mix. A plaid blanket as a prop pulls the whole scene together.

Formula: Red flannel shirt + white tee + high-waisted dark jeans + brown leather boots + denim jacket.

Tip: flannel wrinkles fast, so iron shirts the morning of the shoot and hang them until you change. Roll sleeves to the elbow for a relaxed feel, and match your denim washes across the family — mixed washes read as accidental in photos.

## 9. Metallic Accents for the Holidays

Metallics bring the party to a family photo without requiring a full sequin gown. A little shine in the right places catches the light and makes everyone look celebratory.

Build on a solid base — a black or navy dress, or dark trousers with a cream blouse — then add one metallic piece: a pleated gold skirt, a silver sequin top, or rose-gold accessories. Keep the family in the same base colours with their own metallic accent: a belt, shimmery shoes, a gold headband. One metallic per person is the limit.

Formula: Black midi dress + pleated gold midi skirt (or gold belt) + gold heels + simple gold jewellery.

Tip: metallics reflect your surroundings, so avoid standing directly in front of a mirror or window — the glare will bounce into the lens. Test the shine with a phone photo first, and tone it down if it overpowers your face. Vogue's [fashion coverage](https://www.vogue.com/fashion) shows how stylists balance metallics for the holidays.

## 10. Navy and Silver Coordination

Navy and silver is the cool-toned answer to red and gold: elegant, wintry, and flattering in both daylight and evening light. It suits families who want a refined look without traditional Christmas colours.

Start with a navy midi dress or a navy sweater with tailored trousers. Add silver accessories — a silver belt, drop earrings, metallic silver flats or heels. Dress the family in navy knits and trousers, with silver accents in scarves, shoes, and hair accessories. A silver-grey throw in the background echoes the palette.

Formula: Navy midi dress + silver belt + silver drop earrings + metallic silver heels.

Tip: navy can photograph almost black in low light, so add enough silver and a lighter background to keep its depth visible. A pale wall, snowy window, or light sofa behind you stops navy swallowing the frame.

## 11. Matching Tartan Skirts

Tartan skirts give you the Christmas pattern payoff with a more polished, put-together feel than a full plaid outfit. They work beautifully for mother-daughter matching and sisters posing together.

Choose a tartan in your family palette — red, green, or navy — and wear it as a pleated midi skirt with a solid turtleneck in one of its colours. Add tights and ankle boots. For your daughter, a matching shorter tartan skirt with the same turtleneck keeps the pair linked without being a costume. Keep tops solid and shoes consistent across the group.

Formula: Red tartan pleated midi skirt + forest-green turtleneck + black tights + black ankle boots.

Tip: pleats photograph best pressed and moving, so steam them and take a few walking shots as well as posed ones. If the tartan has a bright line colour — yellow or white — repeat that exact shade in a scarf or accessory so the pattern feels deliberate.

## 12. Festive Fair Isle Knits

Fair Isle knits are pure Christmas nostalgia — the patterned yokes and snowflake motifs read festive instantly, and they are warm enough for outdoor shoots. It shines when the whole family wears the pattern together.

Pick one Fair Isle pattern in shared colours, such as cream, red, and green. Wear it as a Fair Isle sweater dress or crewneck with dark jeans and boots. Give each person the same pattern in their own piece: sweaters, cardigans, hats, or scarves. Keep bottoms dark and simple so the pattern stays the focus.

Formula: Cream Fair Isle sweater + dark jeans + brown boots + matching Fair Isle beanie.

Tip: Fair Isle patterns vary wildly between brands, so buy the family's knits from one collection to keep motif and colours consistent. Avoid pairing Fair Isle with another busy pattern — the two will compete and the photo will look noisy. ## 13. Champagne and Blush Tones

Champagne and blush is the softest palette on this list — romantic, light, and lovely for families who prefer a gentle look over bold Christmas colours. It photographs beautifully in bright, airy rooms.

Start with a champagne satin or chiffon midi dress, layered with a blush pink cardigan or wrap. Add nude heels and delicate rose-gold jewellery. Dress the family in the same soft range: blush knits, champagne shirts, cream trousers. A blush ribbon in your hair or a small bouquet of pale roses ties it together.

Formula: Champagne satin midi dress + blush wrap cardigan + nude heels + rose-gold jewellery.

Tip: pale palettes need contrast, so place the group against a darker background — a green tree, a dark sofa, or a navy wall. Keep makeup a touch stronger than usual — defined brows and a rosy lip — so faces do not disappear into the soft tones.

## 14. Outdoor Snowy Photo Looks

Snowy outdoor photos are magical — and freezing. The trick is an outfit that looks polished in the snow while keeping you warm — shivering, red-nosed photos are not the keepsake anyone wants.

Layer in three stages: a thermal base, a festive mid-layer (a red sweater or plaid dress), and a statement coat in camel, cream, or burgundy. Add a knit beanie, leather gloves, and waterproof boots with good grip. Give the family matching beanies or scarves in one accent colour so the group still reads as coordinated under those coats.

Formula: Thermal base + red knit sweater + camel wool coat + knit beanie + leather gloves + waterproof boots.

Tip: shoot outdoors in short bursts — ten minutes outside, then warm up indoors — and take posed group shots first, before anyone gets cold and rosy. Keep hand warmers in coat pockets, and wipe snow off shoulders between frames so it does not melt into damp patches. ## 15. Coordinated Sequin Tops for Evening Photos

Sequin tops are the easiest way to dress up a family photo after dark: one sparkly piece each on a simple dark base, and the whole group glows. They are perfect for Christmas evening shoots by the tree or before a holiday dinner.

Choose a sequin top in a shared colour — gold, silver, or deep green — with black trousers or a black midi skirt. Keep the rest simple: black heels or flats, minimal jewellery, a sleek hairstyle. Give each person their own sequin piece in the same colour family: a top for you, a skirt for your daughter, a bow tie for your partner.

Formula: Gold sequin top + black tailored trousers + black heels + simple stud earrings.

Tip: sequins and camera flash are a tricky pair, so use soft, diffused light rather than direct flash — or shoot in warm lamp light. One sequin piece per person is the rule; two sparkly items in one outfit fight each other on camera. ## Quick Photo-Day Checklist

- Pick one palette and share it with the whole family a week ahead.
- Lay every outfit out together and take one test photo in daylight.
- Steam or iron everything the night before; hang, do not fold.
- Pack a lint roller, safety pins, blotting paper, and a spare pair of tights.
- Shoot the posed group photo first, then the candid and playful shots.
- Bring a blanket, hot drinks, and hand warmers for outdoor sessions.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What colours look best in Christmas family photos?

Deep, rich tones photograph best: burgundy, forest green, navy, and warm red, balanced with cream or ivory. Avoid neon brights and large areas of pure white, which can blow out in photos, and keep the whole family inside one shared palette of three to four colours.

### Should the whole family wear matching outfits?

Matching exactly can look like a uniform, so coordination works better than identical outfits. Pick one palette and one pattern, then let each person wear a different piece in those colours. The group will look intentional and polished, and everyone will feel comfortable in their own style.

### How do I match outfits without buying everything new?

Start with what everyone already owns in your chosen colours, then buy just one linking piece per person — a scarf, a sweater, or a skirt in the shared shade. Borrow from family, shop secondhand, or rent a statement piece. A single matching accessory can tie together outfits you already have.

### What should women wear for outdoor Christmas photos?

Layer a thermal base under a festive sweater or dress, add a statement wool coat, and finish with a beanie, gloves, and waterproof boots. Choose one accent colour for the family's hats or scarves so the group still looks coordinated under heavy coats, and keep outdoor sessions short.

### When should we take our Christmas family photos?

Book your shoot for late November or early December, when trees are up but schedules are still flexible. Shoot outdoors in the hour before sunset for the softest light, or indoors near a large window in the morning. Always allow time for a test photo before the real session begins.

Pick one palette from this list, gather the family, and take the photo — the best Christmas family photos are the ones where everyone feels comfortable, looks coordinated, and actually had fun taking them.

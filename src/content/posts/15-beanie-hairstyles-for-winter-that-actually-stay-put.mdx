A beanie is the warmest winter accessory you own, and also the one most likely to ruin your hair. The trick is not fighting the hat — it is choosing a hairstyle that works with it. These 15 beanie hairstyles are built to sit flat under a beanie, stay secure through wind and errands, and still look good the moment the hat comes off.

Every style below starts with the same rule: never put a beanie on damp hair. Wet strands set into the shape of the hat, and no amount of shaking fixes that crease. Dry your hair fully, add a little grip at the roots, and pick a style that keeps the crown smooth — then the beanie becomes part of the outfit instead of the enemy of it.

I have worn most of these through real winters — school runs, ski trips, Christmas markets — and the ones that last are always the simplest. Pair them with the right coat and boots, like our [16 classic wool coat outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/16-classic-wool-coat-outfits-for-women/), and you have a full cold-weather look that holds up from the front door to the dinner table.

## 1. The Low Sleek Bun That Survives Any Beanie

The low sleek bun is the single most reliable beanie hairstyle because there is nothing at the crown for the hat to flatten. Brush your hair back into a low ponytail at the nape, twist it into a bun, and pin it with two crossed bobby pins and a small elastic. Keep the bun flat against your head rather than round and high — a flat bun sits under the beanie without creating a lump.

Smooth the top with a pea-sized drop of hair oil or a light pomade before you pull the beanie on. That smoothing layer stops flyaways from frizzing against the knit, and it gives the style a polished finish when the hat comes off indoors. If your hair is fine, tease the ponytail very lightly before twisting so the bun has enough body to hold its shape.

Formula: dry hair + smoothing oil on the crown + low flat bun at the nape + snug beanie pulled low.

Tip: wrap the elastic twice, not three times — an over-tight bun pulls at the hairline and leaves a dent where the beanie band sits.

## 2. The Loose Side Braid for Long Hair

A loose side braid is made for beanies: it keeps long hair contained, it cannot be flattened, and it actually looks better slightly undone. Sweep all your hair over one shoulder, split it into three sections, and braid loosely down to the ends. Secure it with a clear elastic, then gently pull at the edges of the braid to widen it — this is called pancaking, and it makes a thin braid look twice as thick.

The braid sits below the beanie band, so the hat never touches it. When you take the beanie off, the braid is exactly where you left it, and the hair at the crown has only a soft wave from the hat's edge. This is my go-to for windy days, because a braid cannot blow across your face the way loose hair does.

Formula: side part + loose three-strand braid over one shoulder + clear elastic + gently pancaked edges.

Tip: braid on second-day hair, not freshly washed hair — a little natural texture gives the braid grip, while slippery clean hair slides apart under the hat.

## 3. Soft Waves You Can Shake Out After

If you love wearing your hair down, soft waves are the beanie-friendly way to do it. The secret is building the waves low: curl only from the ears down, leaving the top six inches of hair straight. That way the beanie presses on straight hair — which springs back — instead of crushing curls at the crown.

Use a 1.25-inch curling iron, wrap each section away from your face, and hold for eight seconds. Let the curls cool completely before you touch them, then brush through with your fingers for soft movement. A light mist of flexible-hold hairspray keeps the waves from dropping, without the crunch that makes hat hair worse. When the beanie comes off, flip your head upside down, shake at the roots with your fingertips, and the waves fall back into place.

Formula: heat protectant + curls from the ears down only + cool completely + finger-brush + flexible hairspray.

Tip: never curl the top section. Curls at the crown are the first thing a beanie flattens, and they leave a visible dent line that is hard to fix without re-curling.

## 4. The Low Ponytail with a Wrapped Base

A low ponytail sounds too simple to count, but the wrapped base is what makes it beanie-proof and polished. Gather your hair at the nape with a boar-bristle brush so the top stays smooth, secure it with an elastic, then take a thin strand from underneath the ponytail and wrap it around the elastic two or three times. Pin the end underneath with a bobby pin.

The low position means the beanie band sits above the ponytail, never on top of it, so nothing gets squashed or kinked. This style works with straight, wavy, and curly hair, and it takes about two minutes. For a winter walk or a day of errands, pair it with a chunky knit and boots — our [17 stylish winter boots outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/17-stylish-winter-boots-outfits-for-women/) show how the low ponytail finishes a boots-and-knit look.

Formula: boar-bristle brush + low ponytail at the nape + strand wrapped around the elastic + bobby pin underneath.

Tip: tilt your head back slightly while you gather the hair — it stops the ponytail from sitting too high, which is the mistake that makes a beanie bulge at the back.

## 5. The Messy Low Bun for Thick Hair

Thick hair and beanies are a difficult pair, because a high bun creates a lump and loose hair turns into a tangled mess under the knit. The messy low bun solves both problems. Gather your hair at the nape, twist it loosely, and coil it into a bun, pinning as you go — but leave a few face-framing pieces out at the front.

Those loose front pieces are the trick: they soften the look when the beanie comes off, and they hide the flat spot where the hat band sat. Use a scrunchie instead of a tight elastic for the base — it holds thick hair without leaving a kink, and it is gentler on strands that are already dry from winter air. If the bun feels heavy, split it into two smaller twists and pin them side by side.

Formula: scrunchie base at the nape + loose twist + pinned coil + two face-framing pieces left out.

Tip: do not pull the bun tight. A tight bun on thick hair pulls at the roots all day under a hat; a loose coil holds just as well and comes out without a headache.

## 6. Double Dutch Braids for Windy Days

Double Dutch braids are the most secure beanie hairstyle in this list — nothing moves, nothing tangles, and the style survives wind, snow, and a full day under a hat. Part your hair down the middle, then Dutch-braid each side by crossing the strands under instead of over, so the braid sits on top of the hair. Keep the braids snug at the crown and looser toward the ends.

Because the braids lie flat against the head, the beanie slides on smoothly with no lumps. When you take the hat off at the end of the day, undo the braids and you get a bonus: soft, crimped waves that look intentional. This is the style I wear for ski trips and long outdoor days — it pairs perfectly with the outfits in our [15 cute ski trip outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/15-cute-ski-trip-outfits-for-women/).

Formula: centre part + two Dutch braids crossed under + snug at the crown + clear elastics at the ends.

Tip: braid with a little styling cream on your hands. It tames flyaways while you work and stops the braids from fuzzing up under the beanie's knit.

## 7. The Half-Up Twist That Keeps Volume at the Crown

The half-up twist is the answer if you want volume at the crown and a beanie at the same time. Take the top section of hair from temple to temple, twist it once, and pin it at the back of the crown with two crossed bobby pins. Leave the rest of your hair down. The twist lifts the roots just enough that the beanie rests on it instead of pressing your hair flat.

The trick is placement: pin the twist slightly higher than you think, because the beanie will push it down about an inch as you pull it on. Use a texturising spray at the roots before you twist — it gives fine hair the grip it needs to hold the lift. When the hat comes off, remove the pins, shake the roots, and the twist falls into soft volume instead of a flat dent.

Formula: texturising spray at the roots + top section twisted once + pinned high at the crown + rest of the hair left down.

Tip: part your hair on the opposite side from usual before you twist. When you flip it back after removing the beanie, the roots spring up with extra volume — a classic hat-hair fix.

## 8. Curtain Bangs Pinned Back Under a Beanie

Curtain bangs and beanies fight each other: the hat pushes the bangs flat against your forehead, and they come out bent in the wrong direction. The fix is to pin them back before the beanie goes on. Part your bangs in the middle, sweep each side back toward your temples, and secure them with small flat clips hidden under the beanie band.

Blow-dry the bangs first with a round brush, rolling them away from your face, so they hold their curved shape. A quick shot of cool air from the dryer sets the bend. When you take the beanie off, remove the clips, give the bangs a quick finger-comb, and they fall back into their curtain shape instead of sticking to your forehead. If your bangs are still growing out, this trick works even better — the length gives the clips something to hold.

Formula: round-brush blow-dry away from the face + cool-air set + centre part + flat clips at the temples.

Tip: use flat no-crease clips, not bobby pins. Bobby pins leave a visible kink in bangs; flat clips hold without marking the hair.

## 9. The Fishtail Braid That Looks Better After the Hat

A fishtail braid is the beanie hairstyle that genuinely improves while you wear the hat. The gentle pressure of the beanie loosens the braid slightly, and when you take the hat off the braid looks softer and fuller than when you started. Split your hair into two sections, then take a thin strand from the outside of the left section and cross it to the right, and repeat from the other side.

Keep the braid low and over one shoulder so it clears the beanie band entirely. Because a fishtail uses thin strands, it takes a few minutes longer than a regular braid — do it while your coffee brews. Secure the end with a small elastic and pull gently at the edges to widen it. This style works beautifully on medium and long hair, and it holds up through a full day of Christmas shopping.

Formula: low side position + two-section fishtail + thin strands crossed over + gently widened edges.

Tip: fishtails slip apart on very straight, fine hair. Braid on hair with a little dry shampoo worked through it first — the grit gives the strands grip.

## 10. The Silk-Scarf Layer That Stops Static

This is not a hairstyle so much as the one trick that saves every hairstyle in this list: a silk scarf under your beanie. Winter static happens because dry air and wool knit rub against your hair, building up an electric charge that makes strands fly in every direction. A smooth silk layer between your hair and the hat cuts that friction almost completely.

Fold a square silk scarf into a triangle, place the long edge along your hairline, and tie it at the nape under your hair. Then pull the beanie on over it. The scarf also stops the beanie band from leaving a dent across your forehead. Satin-lined beanies work the same way if you would rather buy than layer — and [HairAide's guide to preventing hat hair](https://hairaide.com/how-to-prevent-hat-hair/) explains the friction and moisture causes in detail, including why a smooth lining matters more than the hat's shape.

Formula: silk scarf folded into a triangle + long edge at the hairline + tied at the nape + beanie over the top.

Tip: keep a travel-size anti-static spray or a dryer sheet in your coat pocket. One light pass over the crown after removing the beanie settles any flyaways that escaped the scarf.

## 11. The Low Bubble Ponytail for Fine Hair

Fine hair goes limp under a beanie, but a low bubble ponytail adds volume exactly where the hat cannot crush it — below the band. Gather your hair into a low ponytail at the nape, then add small clear elastics every two inches down the length. Gently pull each section outward between the elastics to puff it into a bubble.

The bubbles hold their shape under a beanie because the elastics lock the volume in place, and the style looks deliberately playful rather than accidental. Tease each section very lightly before you add the next elastic if your hair is especially fine. Three or four bubbles is the sweet spot; more than that starts to look costume-like. This is a great option for holiday parties where you want something more interesting than a plain ponytail.

Formula: low ponytail + clear elastics every two inches + each section gently puffed + light teasing for fine hair.

Tip: spray each bubble lightly with flexible-hold hairspray as you go, not all at the end — it sets the shape section by section and stops the bubbles from deflating.

## 12. Curls Pinned Up in a Loose Pineapple

Curly hair and beanies can work together if you protect the curl pattern instead of crushing it. The loose pineapple does exactly that: gather your curls into a very high, very loose ponytail at the very top of your head — like the top of a pineapple — using a silk scrunchie. The curls pile up above the beanie band, so the hat sits below them and never flattens the curl clumps.

Before you put the beanie on, scrunch a little curl cream through the ends and let the curls settle into the pineapple. When you take the hat off, remove the scrunchie, shake the roots gently, and the curls fall back with their shape intact. A satin-lined beanie helps even more, since satin does not rough up the curl cuticle the way wool does. [Glam's tips for banishing hat hair](https://www.glam.com/1195613/tips-for-banishing-annoying-hat-hair/) also recommend a silk wrap under the hat for the same reason — smooth fabric protects texture.

Formula: curl cream scrunched through ends + loose high pineapple with a silk scrunchie + beanie band below the curls.

Tip: never brush curly hair before the pineapple. Brushing breaks the curl clumps apart, and a beanie on brushed-out curls means frizz, not waves.

## 14. The French Braid Crown for Ski Days

A French braid crown — one braid that wraps around the head like a headband — is the most secure style for a full day on the slopes. Start at one temple, French-braid along the hairline, picking up small sections as you go around the head, and pin the end underneath at the other temple. The braid sits exactly where the beanie band sits, so the hat holds it in place instead of fighting it.

Because the braid follows the hairline, it keeps every strand out of your face, which matters when you are wearing goggles and a helmet over the beanie. It also survives being taken on and off all day — helmet on, helmet off, beanie on, beanie off — without coming undone. Practice this one at home first; it takes about ten minutes once your hands learn the pattern, and it is worth learning before a ski trip.

Formula: start at one temple + French braid along the hairline + pick up small sections + pin the tail under at the other temple.

Tip: braid on slightly damp hair for this one only. A little dampness helps the French braid grip at the hairline — just make sure it is fully dry before the beanie goes on, or the braid will set with a kink.

## 15. The Overnight Braid Waves You Wear Under a Beanie All Day

The easiest beanie hairstyle is the one you make the night before. Before bed, braid your slightly damp hair into two loose braids — one on each side — and sleep on them. In the morning, undo the braids, shake the waves out with your fingers, and add a drop of hair oil to the ends. The waves sit from the mid-lengths down, which is exactly the zone a beanie does not touch.

Pull the beanie on and go. The waves hold all day because they were set overnight, and the hat actually protects them from wind. When you take the beanie off in the evening, the waves are still there — just shake the roots with dry shampoo and you are ready for dinner. Formula: two loose braids on slightly damp hair overnight + undo in the morning + finger-shake + oil on the ends.

Tip: use soft fabric scrunchies for the overnight braids, not elastics. Elastics leave a dent at the ends that shows in the waves; scrunchies do not.

### Your Beanie Hairstyle Checklist

Run through this before you leave the house with a beanie on:

- **Hair 100% dry first** — damp hair sets into the shape of the hat and creases.
- **Grip at the roots** — dry shampoo or texturising spray before the beanie, not after.
- **Keep the crown smooth** — low buns, low ponytails and flat braids beat anything high or voluminous at the top.
- **Silk or satin barrier** — a silk scarf or a satin-lined beanie stops static and forehead dents.
- **Flexible hold, not crunchy** — a light hairspray lets hair bounce back; stiff spray freezes the dent in.
- **Carry a rescue kit** — travel dry shampoo, two bobby pins and a mini brush in your coat pocket fix any hat hair in two minutes.
- **Flip your part after** — parting on the opposite side before the hat gives you instant volume when you flip it back.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I stop my hair from going flat under a beanie?

Start with completely dry hair, add dry shampoo or volumising mousse at the roots before the hat goes on, and choose a low style — a low bun, low ponytail or flat braid — so there is nothing at the crown to flatten. When the beanie comes off, flip your part to the opposite side and shake the roots with your fingertips. A satin-lined beanie also helps, because the smooth lining creates less friction and pressure than bare wool knit.

### What is the best hairstyle to wear under a beanie?

A low sleek bun is the most reliable, because it sits below the beanie band and cannot be crushed. Loose side braids and low ponytails are close behind, and soft waves work well if you only curl from the ears down. Avoid high buns and top knots — they create a lump under the hat and leave a dent when the hat comes off.

### How do I stop static when wearing a beanie?

Static comes from dry winter air and friction between wool knit and your hair. Wear a silk scarf under the beanie or choose a satin-lined beanie, use a drop of hair oil or anti-frizz serum on the mid-lengths before the hat goes on, and keep a dryer sheet or anti-static spray in your pocket for a quick pass over the crown after you take the beanie off.

### Can I wear a beanie with curly hair?

Yes — gather your curls into a loose high pineapple with a silk scrunchie so the curl clumps sit above the beanie band, or wear a low braid that protects the curl pattern. Never brush curly hair before putting a beanie on, and use curl cream on the ends first. A satin-lined beanie is the best choice for curls because satin does not rough up the curl cuticle the way wool does.

### Will a beanie damage my hair?

A beanie will not damage healthy hair on its own, but friction from rough wool can cause breakage and frizz over a whole winter, especially on fine or dry hair. Reduce the friction with a silk scarf or satin lining, avoid pulling the beanie on and off roughly, and keep your hair moisturised with a weekly deep-conditioning treatment through the cold months.

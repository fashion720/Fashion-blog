Long hair in winter is both a gift and a chore: it looks beautiful under a coat, but cold air, wool scarves, and beanies bring static, tangles, and breakage. These 20 long hair hairstyles for winter solve that problem — braids, half-up styles, low buns, and waves that stay put under hats and collars while keeping your ends protected.

Every style below comes with exact steps, the tools and products that make it last, and the cold-weather care that stops winter damage before it starts. I have included five-minute everyday styles, polished work looks, and evening options for the festive season.

## 1. The Classic French Braid Under a Beanie

A French braid is the best beanie hairstyle for long hair, because the braid keeps every strand contained and the beanie slides on and off without creating a nest of tangles. Start at the crown with three small sections, adding hair from each side as you work down, and keep the tension even — not tight enough to hurt, not loose enough to fall apart by lunchtime.

Braid all the way to the ends and secure with a snag-free elastic, then gently pull the outer loops of the braid wider for fullness. Pull your beanie on from front to back so it does not drag the braid upward. Our [15 beanie hairstyles for winter that actually stay put](https://outfitedits.com/posts/15-beanie-hairstyles-for-winter-that-actually-stay-put/) has more hat-proof options if braids are not your thing.

Formula: French braid from the crown + even tension + snag-free elastic + pancake the loops + beanie on front to back.

Tip: braid on second-day hair with a little dry shampoo at the roots — freshly washed hair is too slippery and the braid slides out.

## 2. The Low Braided Ponytail

A low braided ponytail protects your ends from your coat collar while looking far more polished than a plain ponytail. Gather your hair into a low ponytail at the nape, secure it, then braid the tail in a simple three-strand braid and tie the end. Wrap a small strand of hair around the elastic and pin it underneath for a finished look.

This style survives scarves, wind, and hours in a coat without a single tangle at the ends, because the braid stops the hair from whipping around. A drop of hair oil, such as Moroccanoil Treatment, smoothed over the braid keeps flyaways down without making it greasy. It works on straight, wavy, and curly hair alike.

Formula: low nape ponytail + three-strand braid on the tail + hair-wrapped elastic + drop of oil over the braid.

Tip: use a spiral telephone-cord hair tie instead of a tight elastic — it holds without the dent and does not snap long hair when you take it out.

![Woman with long hair in a low braided ponytail with the elastic wrapped in hair](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/2.webp)

## 3. The Half-Up Twist With Face-Framing Pieces

A half-up twist keeps hair out of your face while leaving the length down to show off — the perfect balance for long hair in winter. Take a section from each temple, twist them toward the back, and secure them together with a small claw clip or a clear elastic. Leave two face-framing pieces out at the front, about an inch wide on each side, and curl them loosely away from the face.

Add volume at the crown by gently backcombing the top section before you twist, or by pulling the twisted sections slightly looser after pinning. If you have bangs, this style frames them beautifully — our [25 curtain bangs hairstyles for every face shape](https://outfitedits.com/posts/25-curtain-bangs-hairstyles-for-every-face-shape/) shows how to blend a fringe into a half-up look.

Formula: temple sections twisted back + claw clip + two face-framing pieces + loose curls away from face + crown backcomb.

Tip: twist the sections in opposite directions so they lock together at the back instead of unravelling — one clockwise, one anticlockwise.

![Woman with long hair in a half-up twist with face-framing pieces curled at the front](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/3.webp)

## 4. The Low Chignon Bun

A low chignon is the most elegant long hair hairstyle for winter evenings, and it sits perfectly under a coat collar because it rests at the nape, not on top of your head. Gather your hair low, twist it into a coil, and wrap it into a flat bun, securing it with U-shaped hairpins pushed in from opposite directions. Tuck the ends underneath so nothing pokes out.

Smooth the top with a boar-bristle brush and a light mist of hairspray before you twist — the brush distributes your natural oils and gives the chignon its shine. Leave one thin tendril out on each side for softness, and curl just the ends of the tendrils with a flat iron. This is the bun for holiday dinners and winter weddings.

Formula: low nape gather + twisted coil + flat wrap + U-pins from opposite sides + boar-bristle smoothing + two soft tendrils.

Tip: never use a tight elastic as the base of a chignon — it creates a bump. Use pins only, and the bun will sit flatter and last longer.

![Woman with long hair styled in a low chignon bun at the nape of the neck](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/4.webp)

## 5. Loose Waves Under a Wool Coat

Loose waves are the long hair winter look that needs no updo at all — the waves fall over the shoulders and frame the coat, and the collar actually protects the ends from wind. Curl one-inch sections around a one-and-a-quarter-inch curling iron, alternating directions, then brush through with your fingers once the hair is completely cool. The waves should look soft, not ringleted.

Spray a heat protectant before curling and a flexible-hold spray after — never a strong-hold spray, which makes winter waves crunchy in cold air. If your coat has a high collar, let the front sections fall in front of your shoulders and the back fall behind the collar so the hair does not bunch up.

Formula: heat protectant + 1.25-inch iron + alternate curl directions + finger-brush when cool + flexible-hold spray.

Tip: curl the hair around the iron with the ends left out of the clamp for the last inch — straight ends make waves look modern instead of pageant-perfect.

![Woman with long loose waves in her hair worn over the shoulders of a wool coat](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/5.webp)

## 6. The Bubble Braid

A bubble braid looks intricate but takes five minutes: it is just a ponytail divided into puffs. Gather your hair into a high or mid ponytail, then place elastics every two to three inches down the tail. Gently pull each section outward between the elastics until it forms a round bubble, working from the top down so the bubbles stay even.

Use clear mini elastics for a clean look, or velvet scrunchies for a softer winter feel. A little texturizing spray before you start gives fine hair the grip it needs to hold the bubbles. This style is brilliant in wind — there is nothing to blow apart — and it looks playful with a puffer jacket and a beanie.

Formula: ponytail + elastics every 2-3 inches + pull each section outward + texturizing spray first + clear or velvet elastics.

Tip: make the top bubble the smallest and each one slightly bigger as you go down — a graduated bubble braid looks intentional, not accidental.

![Woman with long hair in a bubble braid with evenly spaced puffs down the ponytail](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/6.webp)

## 7. The Side Braid Over One Shoulder

A side braid brings all your long hair forward over one shoulder, which keeps it off your coat collar and out of the wind — and it photographs beautifully. Sweep all your hair to one side, braid it loosely in a three-strand braid starting behind the ear, and secure the end. Pull a few face-framing strands loose at the front and gently pancake the braid for width.

This is the style for days when your hair is on its last day before wash day, because the looseness hides oil at the roots and the side placement hides flatness at the crown. A silk scarf tied around the base of the braid adds a winter accessory without any extra effort.

Formula: hair swept to one side + loose three-strand braid from behind the ear + pancaked loops + face-framing strands + silk scarf at the base.

Tip: braid over the shoulder opposite your bag strap — the strap will not catch the braid and pull it apart.

![Woman with long hair in a loose side braid draped over one shoulder](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/7.webp)

## 8. The Silk-Scarf Low Ponytail

A silk scarf turns a basic low ponytail into a winter look, and it does real work too: silk creates far less friction than wool or cotton, so your ponytail does not frizz where it rubs against your coat. Gather your hair low at the nape, secure it, then fold a square silk scarf into a band and tie it around the base with the ends hanging down alongside the ponytail.

Smooth the ponytail with a drop of serum first, and choose a scarf in a colour that picks up your coat or your lipstick. The scarf also covers the elastic, so you get a finished look with no extra pins. This is a two-minute style that looks like you tried for twenty.

Formula: low ponytail + smoothing serum + silk scarf folded into a band + tied at the base + ends hanging with the tail.

Tip: tie the scarf over the elastic, not under it — the scarf needs the elastic's grip to stay put, and it hides the elastic at the same time.

![Woman with long hair in a low ponytail tied with a silk scarf at the base](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/8.webp)

## 9. The Messy Low Bun With Loose Tendrils

A messy low bun is the winter workhorse: it keeps long hair fully contained under a coat and scarf, takes three minutes, and looks deliberately undone rather than rushed. Gather your hair at the nape, twist it loosely, wrap it into a bun, and secure it with a large claw clip or a few U-pins. Pull out two thin tendrils at the front and a few pieces at the nape.

The secret is the looseness — a bun pulled too tight looks severe and gives you a headache by midday, while a loose bun with soft pieces looks effortless. A texturizing spray before gathering gives the bun grip, and a quick spritz of flexible hairspray keeps the tendrils from falling into your eyes. The American Academy of Dermatology's [healthy hair tips](https://www.aad.org/public/everyday-care/hair-scalp-care/hair/healthy-hair-tips) recommend looser styles like this one, since tight hairstyles can damage long hair over time.

Formula: loose nape gather + twist and wrap + claw clip or U-pins + two front tendrils + texturizing spray first.

Tip: never wrap the bun around a tight elastic base — twist and pin only, so the bun can be redone in seconds without a hair tie dent.

![Woman with long hair in a messy low bun with loose tendrils framing her face](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/9.webp)

## 10. The Crown Braid

A crown braid — a French or Dutch braid worked around the head like a halo — keeps long hair completely off your neck and collar, which is exactly what you want on the coldest days. Start behind one ear, braid around the hairline adding sections as you go, and finish behind the other ear, tucking the tail under the braid and pinning it flat.

This style takes practice, so do it the first few times on damp hair with a little styling cream for grip, and use a mirror for the back section. Once you can do it in ten minutes, it becomes your best bad-weather style: nothing touches your coat, nothing tangles, and it survives a full day plus a hat.

Formula: braid from behind one ear + around the hairline + tuck and pin the tail + styling cream for grip + practise on damp hair.

Tip: a Dutch braid (underhand) gives a crown braid more definition than a French braid — the braid sits on top of the hair instead of sinking into it.

![Woman with long hair in a crown braid wrapped around her head like a halo](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/10.webp)

## 11. The Fishtail Braid

A fishtail braid looks far more complicated than it is, and on long winter hair it is stunning because the fine, woven texture catches the light. Split your hair into two sections, then take a thin strand from the outside of the left section and cross it to the inside of the right, then repeat from the right to the left. Keep the strands thin — about half an inch — for the classic fishtail look.

Work slowly and keep the tension even, then pancake the braid gently by pulling the outer edges wider once it is secured. A fishtail takes longer than a three-strand braid, so do it on a day when you have ten minutes, not two. It holds beautifully under a scarf because the tight weave does not loosen the way a regular braid does.

Formula: two sections + thin outside strands crossed over + even tension + pancake the edges + snag-free elastic.

Tip: fishtails look best slightly messy, so do not aim for perfection — pull a few strands loose at the front and let the braid be a little uneven.

## 12. The Sleek Low Ponytail

A sleek low ponytail is the most polished long hair winter style, and it works with everything from a work blazer to an evening coat. Brush your hair back with a boar-bristle brush, smoothing the top and sides, and secure it low at the nape. Wrap a strand of hair around the elastic and pin it underneath, then run a flat iron over the tail in slow passes.

A pea-sized amount of smoothing cream, such as Kérastase Discipline, applied before brushing tames flyaways without stiffness, and a final mist of shine spray on the tail gives it a mirror finish. Part your hair in the centre for a sharp, editorial look, or in a deep side part for something softer. This is the style to choose when your outfit is doing the talking.

Formula: boar-bristle brush back + low nape secure + hair-wrapped elastic + flat-iron the tail + smoothing cream + shine spray.

Tip: brush the hair back while your head is tilted slightly forward — it pulls the top smooth without creating a bump at the crown.

![Woman with long hair in a sleek low ponytail with a smooth glossy finish](/images/content/20-long-hair-hairstyles-for-winter/12.webp)

## 13. The Half-Up Claw Clip Twist

The claw clip is back, and the half-up twist is the easiest way to wear it with long hair in winter. Gather the top half of your hair, twist it upward once, and clip it with a large claw clip so the ends cascade over the top of the clip. Leave the bottom half down and wavy or straight — either works, because the twist is the feature.

Choose a clip with strong teeth and a matte finish; cheap shiny clips slip out of thick hair by noon. A large clip, at least four inches long, holds long hair without snapping, and a tortoiseshell or dark colour looks intentional rather than accidental. This style takes thirty seconds and survives a coat, a scarf, and a car seat.

Formula: top half gathered + one upward twist + large matte claw clip + ends cascading over the clip + bottom half left down.

Tip: twist the section upward, not downward — an upward twist lifts the crown and the clip sits higher, which flatters your face shape.

## 14. The Braided Headband Half-Up

A braided headband gives you the look of a headband with none of the headache, and it keeps the front of your long hair off your face all day. Take a section of hair from behind one ear, braid it in a simple three-strand braid, then bring it across the top of your head like a headband and pin it behind the other ear. Repeat from the other side if you want a double band.

Leave the rest of your hair down in soft waves or straight, and pull a few face-framing pieces loose in front of the braid. This style works beautifully with a beanie, because the braid anchors the front while the hat sits behind it. It is also the perfect way to hide roots between colour appointments.

Formula: braid from behind one ear + across the crown + pin behind the other ear + rest of hair down + face-framing pieces loose.

Tip: braid the headband section on slightly damp hair with a little mousse — dry hair slips and the braid loosens within an hour.

## 15. The Low Twisted Bun for Work

A low twisted bun is the professional version of the messy bun: same nape placement, but smooth, tight, and deliberate. Part your hair in the centre or a neat side part, brush it smooth, divide it into two sections at the nape, twist each section inward, then wrap the two twists around each other into a bun and pin it flat with U-pins.

A smoothing serum and a fine-tooth comb are essential here — the bun's beauty is its clean lines, and any bumps show. Finish with a medium-hold hairspray and a quick pass of the comb over the top to catch flyaways. It pairs with a blazer, a turtleneck, and a long coat, and it stays put through a full workday plus a commute. For the rest of the work outfit, our [17 rich burgundy winter outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/17-rich-burgundy-winter-outfits-for-women/) show how a polished bun completes a winter work look.

Formula: neat part + smooth brush + two inward twists + wrapped bun + U-pins + serum + medium-hold spray.

Tip: twist both sections in the same direction before wrapping them around each other — opposite twists fight and the bun unravels.

## 16. The Overnight Heatless Waves

Heatless waves are the winter gift your long hair has been waiting for: no hot tools, no extra dryness, and you wake up styled. On damp hair before bed, divide it into four sections and braid each one loosely, or twist each section around a silk heatless curling rod and secure the ends. Sleep on a silk pillowcase, and in the morning undo the braids and shake the waves out with your fingers.

A pea-sized amount of leave-in conditioner before braiding keeps the waves soft instead of crunchy, and a light oil on the ends seals in moisture overnight. This method works best on hair that is about eighty percent dry when you braid it — soaking wet hair will still be damp in the morning, and fully dry hair will not hold the wave.

Formula: damp hair + four loose braids or a silk rod + silk pillowcase + leave-in conditioner + finger-shake in the morning.

Tip: never brush heatless waves — brushing turns waves into frizz. Use your fingers only, and add a drop of oil to your palms first.

## 17. The Double Dutch Braids Under a Beanie

Double Dutch braids are the most secure long hair style for active winter days — skiing, long walks, windy commutes — because two braids distribute the weight and nothing moves. Part your hair down the centre, Dutch-braid each side from the hairline to the nape, then continue each braid to the ends and secure them. Tuck the ends under your coat collar or let them hang in front of your shoulders.

Dutch braids cross under rather than over, so they sit raised on the head and hold their shape under a beanie better than French braids. Pull your beanie on from front to back and it will not disturb the braids at all. Our [15 cute ski trip outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/15-cute-ski-trip-outfits-for-women/) pair perfectly with this practical style.

Formula: centre part + Dutch braid each side + braid to the ends + secure + beanie on front to back.

Tip: braid with a little styling cream on your hands — it gives grip, stops flyaways, and keeps the braids neat for hours in wind and snow.

## 18. The Silk-Lined Beanie Waves

This is a care style as much as a hairstyle: the secret is the beanie, not the hair. A regular acrylic beanie creates friction and static every time it moves, but a silk- or satin-lined beanie lets long hair slide instead of catching. Wear your hair down in loose waves or a low loose braid, and put on a lined beanie — your waves will still be there when you take it off.

If you do not own a lined beanie, wear a silk scarf under a regular one: fold the scarf into a triangle, place it over your hair, then pull the beanie on over it. For static that is already there, the fixes in [these tips for combatting static hair in winter](https://www.women.com/1509707/best-tips-combatting-static-hair-winter/) work fast — leave-in conditioner on the lengths and a silk pillowcase at night make the biggest difference of all.

Formula: loose waves or low braid + silk-lined beanie (or silk scarf under a regular beanie) + leave-in conditioner on the lengths.

Tip: never pull a beanie straight off upward — slide it forward and off, so it does not drag your hair up into a static cloud.

## 19. The Romantic Low Side Bun for Evenings

A low side bun is the evening version of the chignon: it sits low and to one side, just above the shoulder, and it shows off earrings and a neckline in a way a centre bun cannot. Gather your hair to one side at the nape, twist it loosely, coil it into a soft bun, and pin it with U-pins and a decorative pin or two. Leave the front sections soft and curled loosely around your face.

Curl the lengths with a large-barrel iron before gathering, so the bun has texture instead of sitting flat, and pull a few pieces loose after pinning for a romantic finish. A spritz of shine spray gives the bun an evening glow. Pair it with a festive dress or coat — our [15 glamorous holiday dress outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/15-glamorous-holiday-dress-outfits-for-women/) show how this kind of polished hair completes an evening look.

Formula: side gather at the nape + loose twist and coil + U-pins + decorative pin + pre-curled lengths + soft face pieces.

Tip: place the bun on the opposite side from your part — the diagonal line across the back of your head is more flattering than a bun directly under the part.

## 20. The Protective Braided Bun for Cold Snaps

On the coldest days, the kindest thing you can do for long hair is put it away completely: a braided bun tucks every end inside, so wind, wool collars, and freezing air never touch the most fragile part of your hair. Braid your hair in a single three-strand braid from the nape down, then coil the braid into a flat bun at the nape and pin it securely.

Before braiding, work a leave-in conditioner and a drop of oil through the lengths — the bun then acts like a deep-conditioning treatment all day, sealed in by your coat and hat. Undo it in the evening and your ends will feel softer than they did in the morning. This is the style to choose whenever the temperature drops below freezing or the wind picks up.

Formula: leave-in conditioner + oil on the lengths + single braid + coil into a flat bun + pin securely + undo in the evening.

Tip: never put wet hair into a braided bun in freezing weather — wet hair can freeze and snap. Dry it fully first, then braid.

### Your Long Hair Winter Checklist

Before the coldest weeks arrive, run through this list:

- **Braid before the beanie** — a braid or low bun stops tangles and breakage under hats and scarves.
- **Silk or satin lining** — a lined beanie or a silk scarf under your hat cuts friction and static dramatically.
- **Leave-in conditioner daily** — winter air strips moisture, so condition the lengths every morning of hat season.
- **Loose over tight** — tight ponytails and buns cause breakage; choose loose styles and snag-free ties.
- **Oil the ends weekly** — a drop of hair oil on the ends seals in moisture and stops split ends spreading.
- **Dry hair before going out** — never leave the house with wet hair in freezing weather; it can freeze and snap.
- **Silk pillowcase at night** — it removes hours of overnight friction and keeps morning styles intact.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I stop my long hair from getting static in winter?

Static comes from dry air plus friction from hats, scarves, and coats. The fix is moisture and less friction: use a leave-in conditioner on the lengths every morning, switch to a silk-lined beanie or a silk scarf under your hat, and use a metal comb instead of plastic. A drop of hair oil smoothed over the ends also weighs flyaways down.

### What is the best hairstyle for long hair under a beanie?

A French braid or double Dutch braids, because the hair is fully contained and the beanie slides on and off without tangling it. A low braided ponytail or a low bun works too. Avoid wearing long hair loose under a beanie — the friction creates a tangled, static mess by the time you take the hat off.

### How can I protect my long hair from cold weather damage?

Keep the ends tucked away in braids or buns, condition the lengths daily, oil the ends weekly, and never go outside with wet hair in freezing temperatures. Wash less often in winter if you can, use lukewarm water instead of hot, and sleep on a silk pillowcase to cut overnight friction.

### Can I wear my long hair down in winter?

Yes — loose waves look beautiful under a wool coat, and the collar protects the ends from wind. The trick is preparation: heat protectant before styling, a flexible-hold spray after, and a silk-lined beanie or scarf if you are wearing a hat. On windy or snowy days, switch to a braid to avoid tangles.

### How often should I trim long hair in winter?

Every eight to twelve weeks is enough for long hair, but do not skip the winter trim — cold air and friction make split ends travel up the strand faster. Ask your stylist to dust the ends, taking off just a quarter of an inch, which removes damage without losing length.

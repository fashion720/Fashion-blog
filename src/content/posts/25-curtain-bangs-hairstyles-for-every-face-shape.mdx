Curtain bangs are the rare haircut that suits almost everyone — the trick is knowing which version suits your face shape. These 25 curtain bangs hairstyles cover every face shape, from round to long, with the exact lengths, layers, and styling steps that make each one work. No guessing, no salon-surprise regret.

You will learn how long your bangs should be for your face shape, which cuts to pair them with, and how to style them in under five minutes with a round brush and a dryer. Every style below includes a formula you can show your stylist and a tip that keeps the bangs behaving between cuts.

If you are still deciding whether fringe is for you, start with a longer, softer version — it grows out gracefully and you can always go shorter. And if you want the full look, our [20 classy elegant winter outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/20-classy-elegant-winter-outfits-to-pin-and-wear/) pair beautifully with a fresh curtain-bang cut.

## 1. Classic Curtain Bangs for a Round Face

For a round face, the classic curtain bang is your best friend — the centre part and longer sides create vertical lines that lengthen the face instead of widening it. Ask your stylist for bangs that start at the cheekbone and taper to the jawline, with the shortest piece in the centre hitting just below the eyebrows. The length is the whole trick: too short and the bangs widen your face, too long and they disappear into the layers.

Style them by blow-drying each side away from your face with a medium round brush, and finish with a light texturising spray rather than a heavy cream. Keep the rest of your hair long with soft layers, because length below the shoulders balances the roundness. Avoid blunt, straight-across bangs — they cut the face in half and emphasise width.

Formula: centre part + cheekbone-to-jawline length + round-brush blow-dry + long layered hair + texturising spray.

Tip: ask for the bangs to be cut dry, not wet — wet bangs shrink as they dry, and a dry cut shows your stylist exactly where the fringe will sit.

## 2. Long Curtain Bangs with Layers for Round Faces

Long curtain bangs are the low-commitment version for round faces — they start at the cheekbone and blend so gradually into the layers that they read as face-framing, not fringe. Ask for the shortest piece to hit the cheekbone and the longest to reach the chin, cut with a razor or point-cutting so the ends stay soft. The layers should start at the chin and fall through the lengths, which keeps the silhouette long and narrow.

This style is the easiest to grow out, because the bangs are already long enough to tuck behind your ears on lazy days. Blow-dry with a large round brush, rolling the bangs back and away from the face, and add a drop of shine serum to the ends. It works on straight, wavy, and slightly curly hair, and it suits anyone who wants the curtain-bang look without the daily styling.

Formula: cheekbone-to-chin length + point-cut ends + chin-length layers + large round brush + shine serum.

Tip: if your bangs keep falling into your eyes, they were cut too short — the fix is not more product, it is two more weeks of growth and a reshape.

![Woman with long curtain bangs and layers for a round face](/images/content/25-curtain-bangs-hairstyles-for-every-face-shape/2.webp)

## 3. Curtain Bangs with a Shag for Round Faces

A shag cut with curtain bangs is the edgiest option for a round face, and it works because the choppy layers and volume at the crown add height — and height lengthens a round face. Ask for a modern shag with curtain bangs that hit the cheekbone, layers that start at the crown, and plenty of texture through the ends. The cut should feel light and piecey, never heavy at the sides.

Style it with a diffuser if your hair is wavy, or rough-dry it and add a texturising spray for that lived-in finish. The bangs should be styled with a round brush, curving away from the face, so they frame rather than cover. This is a great choice if your hair is thick, because the shag removes weight, and it pairs well with [short haircuts for women with a round face](https://outfitedits.com/posts/20-short-haircuts-for-women-with-a-round-face/) if you want to go shorter later.

Formula: modern shag + cheekbone curtain bangs + crown layers + texturising spray + round-brush fringe.

Tip: a shag needs a texturising spray, not a smoothing serum — serum flattens the layers and kills the piecey texture that makes the cut work.

## 4. Wispy Curtain Bangs for an Oval Face

An oval face can wear almost any fringe, so the wispy curtain bang is about softness rather than correction. Ask for bangs that are feather-light, starting at the eyebrows and tapering to the cheekbones, with the ends thinned out so you can see through them slightly. The wispy texture keeps the fringe from feeling heavy on a face that does not need any slimming or widening.

Style them by blow-drying with a small round brush, curving each side gently outwards, and finish with a pea-sized amount of styling cream smoothed through the ends. The rest of the hair can be anything — a bob, a lob, long layers — because the bangs are the lightest version and they adapt. This is the best first curtain bang if you have never had fringe before, since the wispy ends are forgiving while they grow.

Formula: feather-light ends + eyebrow-to-cheekbone length + small round brush + styling cream + any length below.

Tip: thin the ends with point-cutting, not thinning shears — shears can leave a frizzy, blunt line, while point-cutting keeps the fringe soft and piecey.

## 5. Curtain Bangs with a Blunt Bob for Oval Faces

Pairing curtain bangs with a blunt bob is the sharpest look in this guide — the straight, clean bob gives structure, while the soft curtain bangs keep it from feeling severe. Ask for a bob that ends at the jawline or just below, with curtain bangs that start at the eyebrows and sweep to the cheekbones. The contrast is the point: blunt below, feathered above.

Blow-dry the bob smooth with a paddle brush and a flat iron on the ends, then shape the bangs separately with a round brush, curving them away from the face. A shine serum over the whole head ties the two textures together. This style is ideal if your hair is naturally straight or slightly wavy, and it looks polished with minimal effort — the bob does the work, the bangs do the framing.

Formula: jawline blunt bob + eyebrow-to-cheekbone bangs + paddle brush + flat iron on ends + shine serum.

Tip: keep the bangs slightly longer than you think — on a blunt bob, short bangs can look like a mistake, while longer bangs look like a deliberate choice.

![Woman with curtain bangs and a blunt bob for an oval face](/images/content/25-curtain-bangs-hairstyles-for-every-face-shape/5.webp)

## 6. Long Curtain Bangs with a Middle Part for Oval Faces

Long curtain bangs with a dead-centre part are the most effortless look for an oval face — they fall open like curtains, frame the cheekbones, and need almost no styling. Ask for bangs that start at the nose and taper to the collarbone, blending into long layers. The middle part is non-negotiable here: it is what creates the symmetrical frame that flatters an oval face.

Air-drying works surprisingly well for this style if your hair is wavy, but a quick round-brush blow-dry gives it polish. The key is keeping the bangs long enough to tuck behind your ears when you want them gone — that is what makes this the lowest-maintenance curtain bang in the whole guide. A texturising spray adds movement without weight, and the look pairs beautifully with [choppy bob haircuts](https://outfitedits.com/posts/20-choppy-bob-haircuts-for-women-in-2026/) if you ever want to shorten the lengths.

Formula: nose-to-collarbone length + centre part + long layers + round-brush blow-dry + texturising spray.

Tip: find your natural centre part by combing wet hair straight back and letting it fall — forcing a part where your hair does not want one means a daily fight.

## 7. Soft Curtain Bangs for a Square Face

A square face has a strong jawline, so the goal is softness — and soft curtain bangs are the best tool for it. Ask for bangs that start at the eyebrows, taper to the jawline, and are cut with plenty of texture so they fall in feathery pieces rather than a solid sheet. The softness at the forehead balances the angles at the jaw, and the longer side pieces draw the eye down in a gentle curve.

Style them with a round brush, curving the ends away from the face and slightly under at the jawline. Keep the rest of the hair in soft waves rather than pin-straight, because waves add more softness where a square face needs it. Avoid blunt bangs and sharp, geometric cuts — they echo the jawline instead of softening it. A light-hold spray keeps the fringe moving without stiffness.

Formula: eyebrow-to-jawline length + feathered texture + round-brush curves + soft waves below + light-hold spray.

Tip: the bangs should be longest at the sides, not the centre — long side pieces soften the jaw, while a short centre piece draws attention to the forehead.

## 8. Curtain Bangs with a Layered Lob for Square Faces

A layered lob — a long bob that ends between the chin and the shoulders — is the perfect partner for curtain bangs on a square face, because the layers break up the jawline from every angle. Ask for a lob with layers starting at the chin, and curtain bangs that sweep from the eyebrows to the jawline. The layers should be soft and blended, not choppy, so the whole cut reads as one flowing shape.

Blow-dry the lob with a large round brush, turning the ends under slightly, and shape the bangs separately so they fall open over the forehead. A smoothing cream on the lengths keeps the lob sleek, while a texturising spray on the bangs keeps them piecey. This cut is ideal for work — it looks polished in an office and needs only a five-minute blow-dry, which is why it pairs so well with our [easy winter office outfits](https://outfitedits.com/posts/17-easy-winter-office-outfits-for-women-to-plan-ahead/).

Formula: chin-to-shoulder lob + chin-start layers + eyebrow-to-jawline bangs + large round brush + smoothing cream.

Tip: keep the lob at or below the jawline — a bob that ends exactly at the jaw draws a horizontal line across the widest part of a square face.

## 9. Side-Swept Curtain Bangs for a Square Jawline

Side-swept curtain bangs are a clever variation for a square jawline, because the diagonal line across the forehead breaks up the symmetry of a strong jaw. Ask for bangs that are shorter on one side and longer on the other, with the longest piece reaching the cheekbone, and a part that sits slightly off-centre. The asymmetry is the whole point — it softens the face by refusing to mirror it.

Style them by blow-drying the shorter side towards the forehead and the longer side sweeping across, using a round brush to curve both away from the face. A small amount of styling cream keeps the sweep in place without stiffness. This version works on any length below, from a pixie to long layers, and it is the best curtain bang if you wear glasses, since the sweep clears the frames.

Formula: off-centre part + asymmetric lengths + cheekbone longest piece + round-brush sweep + styling cream.

Tip: the sweep should start at the shorter side and move towards the longer side — sweeping the wrong way fights your part and collapses by lunchtime.

## 10. Feathered Curtain Bangs for a Heart Face

A heart-shaped face is widest at the forehead and narrowest at the chin, so feathered curtain bangs are ideal — they soften the forehead while the longer side pieces add width where the chin needs it. Ask for bangs that are light and feathered, starting at the eyebrows and tapering to the chin, with the ends thinned so they fall in soft, separate pieces. The feathering is what makes this work: a solid fringe would add bulk to an already wide forehead.

Blow-dry the bangs with a round brush, curving each side outwards, and keep the rest of the hair in soft waves that start at the chin — waves at the jawline add the width a heart face needs at the bottom. A texturising spray gives the fringe its piecey finish, and [Who What Wear's curtain bangs guide](https://www.whowhatwear.com/curtain-bangs) shows how the feathered version photographs on different face shapes.

Formula: feathered ends + eyebrow-to-chin length + round-brush outward curves + chin-level waves + texturising spray.

Tip: keep the chin-length waves, not the bangs, doing the widening — if the bangs themselves are too wide at the bottom, the forehead looks even broader.

## 11. Curtain Bangs with a Chin-Length Bob for Heart Faces

A chin-length bob with curtain bangs is the most balanced cut for a heart face, because the bob ends exactly where the face needs width — at the jaw. Ask for a bob that hits the chin with soft, textured ends, and curtain bangs that start at the eyebrows and sweep to the cheekbones. The bob's width at the chin counterbalances the forehead, and the bangs soften the hairline.

Blow-dry the bob with a round brush, turning the ends slightly under, and shape the bangs so they fall open on either side of the centre part. A smoothing cream keeps the bob sleek without flattening it, and a light-hold spray holds the bangs in place. This is a great cut if your hair is fine, because the chin length makes it look thicker, and the bangs add style without needing volume tricks.

Formula: chin-length bob + textured ends + eyebrow-to-cheekbone bangs + round brush + smoothing cream.

Tip: ask for the bob to be cut with the ends textured, not blunt — blunt ends at the chin can look heavy on a narrow jaw, while textured ends move and soften.

## 12. Long Curtain Bangs with Soft Waves for Heart Faces

Long curtain bangs with soft waves are the most romantic option for a heart face, and they work because the waves start low — at the chin — where the face needs fullness. Ask for bangs that begin at the cheekbone and taper to the collarbone, blending into long layers, and keep the top of the hair smooth so the volume sits at the bottom, not the forehead.

Curl the lengths with a 32 mm iron in large sections, alternating directions for a natural fall, and brush the curls out gently so they become waves rather than ringlets. Style the bangs with a round brush, curving them away from the face, and finish with a flexible-hold spray. This look is at its best on medium to long hair, and it photographs beautifully because the waves frame the jawline.

Formula: cheekbone-to-collarbone bangs + long layers + 32 mm iron waves + round-brush fringe + flexible-hold spray.

Tip: keep the roots flat and the waves low — volume at the crown widens the forehead, which is the opposite of what a heart face needs.

## 13. Curtain Bangs for a Long Face

A long face needs width, not length, so curtain bangs for a long face should be shorter and wider than the standard version. Ask for bangs that hit the eyebrows at their shortest point, sweep to the cheekbones, and are cut with a wider part — the fringe should cover more of the forehead than usual. The extra coverage shortens the face visually, which is exactly the goal.

Pair them with a cut that adds width at the sides: a lob, a shag, or long hair with layers that start at the chin. Avoid very long, straight hair with long bangs — it stretches the face further. Blow-dry the bangs with a round brush, curving them outwards for volume, and add a texturising spray at the sides for fullness. A side part can work too, but the centre part gives the most symmetrical shortening effect.

Formula: eyebrow-length shortest piece + wider fringe + cheekbone sweep + side volume + round-brush outward curves.

Tip: the bangs should be the widest part of the cut — if they are narrow and long, they will make a long face look longer, not shorter.

![Woman with curtain bangs for a long face and a layered lob](/images/content/25-curtain-bangs-hairstyles-for-every-face-shape/13.webp)

## 14. Curtain Bangs with a Shaggy Midi Cut for Long Faces

A shaggy midi cut — shoulder-length with choppy layers — is the best partner for curtain bangs on a long face, because the layers add width at exactly the right height. Ask for a midi cut with layers starting at the cheekbones, curtain bangs that hit the eyebrows and sweep to the cheekbones, and plenty of texture through the ends. The cut should feel wide and piecey, never long and flat.

Style it by rough-drying with a diffuser or your fingers, then adding texturising spray and scrunching the layers for movement. The bangs get a quick round-brush pass to curve them away from the face. This is a low-maintenance cut that looks better slightly messy, and it is ideal if your hair is wavy, because the natural texture does half the styling for you.

Formula: shoulder-length shaggy midi + cheekbone layers + eyebrow-length bangs + texturising spray + round-brush fringe.

Tip: never flat-iron a shaggy midi smooth — the flatness kills the width that makes the cut flatter a long face.

## 15. Curtain Bangs with Curls for a Long Face

Curls are a long face's best friend, and curtain bangs with curls are the most flattering combination in this guide for that face shape. The curls add width at the sides, the bangs shorten the forehead, and together they balance the face from every angle. Ask for curtain bangs that hit the eyebrows and sweep to the cheekbones, with the rest of the hair cut in long layers that curl easily.

If your hair is naturally curly, ask your stylist to cut the bangs dry and slightly longer than you want — curly bangs shrink as they dry, and a dry cut prevents the dreaded too-short surprise. Style with a curl cream and a diffuser, scrunching the curls up towards the roots, and let the bangs fall naturally rather than forcing them straight. [Bangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangs_(hair)) have been styled with curls for decades, and the curtain version is the most forgiving of all.

Formula: eyebrow-length bangs + long layers + curl cream + diffuser + dry-cut for curly hair.

Tip: cut curly curtain bangs at least 2 cm longer than your target length — curls spring up as they dry, and you can always trim more, but you cannot add length back.

## 16. Curtain Bangs with a High Ponytail

Curtain bangs with a high ponytail are the perfect everyday look — the ponytail lifts the face, and the bangs soften it, so you get polish without stiffness. Gather your hair at the crown and secure it with a snag-free elastic, then wrap a small section around the base to hide it. The bangs stay down and frame your face, which is what stops a high ponytail from looking severe.

Style the bangs first with a round brush, curving each side away from the face, and add a little texturising spray at the roots of the ponytail for grip. Curl the ponytail itself in large sections for bounce, or leave it straight for a sleeker finish. This look works on every face shape because the bangs do the framing and the ponytail does the lifting, and it takes about five minutes once you have the routine down.

Formula: round-brush bangs first + high crown ponytail + hair-wrapped base + texturising spray + curled or straight ponytail.

Tip: tilt your chin down while you gather the ponytail — it pulls the height up and stops the sides from sagging, which keeps the bangs sitting where they should.

## 17. Curtain Bangs with a Messy Bun

A messy bun with curtain bangs is the off-duty look that still looks intentional, and it is the fastest style in this guide. Gather your hair into a loose high bun, pin it with U-shaped pins, and pull out a few pieces around the hairline for softness. The curtain bangs stay down and frame the face, which is what makes a messy bun look styled rather than accidental.

The key is the balance: the bun should be loose and textured, but the bangs should be shaped with a round brush so they look deliberate. A texturising spray through the bun gives it grip, and a light-hold spray over the bangs keeps them in place without stiffness. This is the ideal style for second-day hair, and it pairs beautifully with a [classic wool coat](https://outfitedits.com/posts/16-classic-wool-coat-outfits-for-women/) and a scarf on cold days.

Formula: loose high bun + U-shaped pins + shaped curtain bangs + texturising spray + light-hold spray.

Tip: build the bun on day-old hair with dry shampoo at the roots — clean hair slips out of a messy bun within an hour, but second-day hair holds all day.

## 18. Curtain Bangs and a Wolf Cut

The wolf cut — a shag-mullet hybrid with heavy layers and volume at the crown — is the boldest pairing for curtain bangs, and it works because the bangs are the softest part of an otherwise edgy cut. Ask for a wolf cut with curtain bangs that hit the cheekbones and taper to the jawline, layers that start at the crown, and plenty of texture through the lengths. The cut should feel wild and piecey, with the bangs providing the frame.

Style it with a texturising spray and a diffuser if your hair is wavy, or rough-dry and scrunch if it is straight. The bangs get a round-brush pass to curve them away from the face, which keeps them from blending into the chaos of the layers. This is a statement cut that suits thick hair best, because the layers remove weight, and it is the most low-maintenance style here — it looks better the messier it gets.

Formula: wolf cut + cheekbone-to-jawline bangs + crown layers + texturising spray + round-brush fringe.

Tip: a wolf cut needs a texturising spray, not a smoothing serum — serum flattens the layers and turns the cut into a plain shag.

## 19. Curtain Bangs with a Pixie Cut

Curtain bangs on a pixie cut are the most surprising combination in this guide, and they work because the longer fringe softens the shortness everywhere else. Ask for a pixie with length on top — at least 8 to 10 cm at the front — and curtain bangs that sweep from the eyebrows to the cheekbones, with the sides and back kept short and textured. The contrast between the short back and the longer fringe is what makes the cut feel modern rather than boyish.

Style the bangs with a small round brush or your fingers and a pea-sized amount of styling cream, sweeping them open on either side of the centre part. The rest of the pixie needs only a little texture paste scrunched through the top. This cut is ideal if you want short hair but are nervous about losing femininity — the curtain bangs keep the softness, and the pixie keeps the edge.

Formula: pixie with 8-10 cm front length + eyebrow-to-cheekbone bangs + styling cream + texture paste + centre part.

Tip: keep the fringe longer than you think on a pixie — short bangs on a short cut can look accidental, while longer bangs look like a deliberate style choice.

## 20. Curtain Bangs with Long Straight Hair

## 21. Curtain Bangs with Beach Waves

A French bob with curtain bangs is the chicest short look in this guide — the bob ends at the cheekbone or just below, the bangs sweep open from the eyebrows, and the whole cut feels Parisian without trying. ## 23. Growing Out Curtain Bangs Gracefully

Growing out curtain bangs is the easiest fringe grow-out there is, because the bangs are already long and parted in the middle — they blend into the layers instead of hanging in your eyes. The trick is to stop trimming the centre and let the shortest piece grow past your eyebrows, while your stylist trims only the side pieces so they keep blending into the layers. Every six to eight weeks, ask for a dusting of the sides only, never the centre.

While they grow, style the bangs with a round brush, curving them away from the face, and use a small clip or bobby pin to hold them back on days when they will not behave. A texturising spray adds grip and stops the growing fringe from falling flat. Within three to four months, the bangs will be long enough to tuck behind your ears, and you will have face-framing layers instead of fringe — no awkward in-between stage.

Formula: no centre trims + side dusting every 6-8 weeks + round-brush styling + clips for bad days + texturising spray.

Tip: resist the urge to trim the centre yourself — one impatient snip resets the grow-out by a month, and the sides-only trim is what makes it painless.

## 24. Curtain Bangs for Curly Hair

Curtain bangs on curly hair are the most misunderstood version in this guide — people assume curls and fringe do not mix, but a well-cut curly curtain bang is one of the most flattering styles there is. The key is the cut: your stylist must cut the bangs dry, curl by curl, and leave them at least 2 cm longer than the target length, because curls spring up as they dry. The bangs should start at the eyebrows when dry and taper to the cheekbones.

Style with a curl cream scrunched through damp hair and a diffuser, lifting the roots gently so the bangs fall open rather than clumping together. Never brush curly bangs dry — it turns them into a frizz halo. A light curl-defining gel on the bangs keeps them separated and bouncy, and the rest of the curls can be left to do their own thing. This is the lowest-maintenance curtain bang once the cut is right, because the curls create the shape for you.

Formula: dry curl-by-curl cut + 2 cm extra length + curl cream + diffuser + curl-defining gel on bangs.

Tip: wash curly bangs less often than the rest of your hair — a quick rinse and re-scrunch refreshes them without a full wash, and over-washing kills the curl pattern.

## 25. Curtain Bangs for Fine Hair

Fine hair and curtain bangs are a perfect match, because the bangs add the illusion of fullness at the front without needing volume tricks. Ask for bangs that are cut with a slightly wider part — taking hair from further back on the crown — so the fringe has enough density to look substantial. The shortest piece should hit the eyebrows, tapering to the cheekbones, and the ends should be point-cut for softness.

Style the bangs with a small round brush and a root-lifting spray applied to damp hair, blow-drying the bangs up and away from the face for lift. A texturising spray through the lengths adds grip, and a dry shampoo at the roots on day two keeps the bangs from going flat and greasy. Avoid heavy creams and oils on fine bangs — they weigh the fringe down within an hour. This is the best curtain bang if your hair is fine, because it creates fullness exactly where fine hair usually looks thinnest.

Formula: wider fringe section + eyebrow-to-cheekbone length + root-lifting spray + small round brush + dry shampoo on day two.

Tip: take the bangs from further back on the crown — a narrow fringe on fine hair looks wispy and sparse, while a wider section gives the bangs real presence.

### Your Curtain Bangs Checklist

Before your cut, and every morning after, run through this list:

- **Know your face shape** — round needs length, square needs softness, heart needs feathering, long needs width, oval can wear anything.
- **Ask for a dry cut** — wet bangs shrink as they dry, and a dry cut shows exactly where the fringe will sit.
- **Style the bangs first** — blow-dry them while they are wettest, before the rest of your hair, or they will dry flat and refuse to behave.
- **Round brush, away from the face** — the curve is what makes curtain bangs open like curtains instead of falling straight down.
- **Texturising spray, not heavy cream** — cream weighs bangs down, while spray keeps them piecey and moving.
- **Trim every 4-6 weeks** — a quick fringe dusting keeps the length right without a full haircut.
- **Dry shampoo on day two** — bangs go greasy first, and a quick spray at the roots buys you an extra day.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do curtain bangs suit every face shape?

Yes, with the right length — round faces need cheekbone-to-jawline bangs for length, square faces need soft feathered bangs to balance the jaw, heart faces need feathered bangs with chin-level waves, long faces need shorter, wider bangs for width, and oval faces can wear any version. The cut matters more than the fringe itself, so show your stylist a photo of the exact length you want for your face shape.

### How do I style curtain bangs in the morning?

Blow-dry them first, while they are at their wettest, using a round brush to curve each side away from your face. It takes about two minutes. Finish with a texturising spray or a pea-sized amount of styling cream, and never blow-dry them from above with the nozzle pointing down — that flattens them. On lazy days, a velcro roller on each side for five minutes does the same job.

### How often do curtain bangs need trimming?

Every four to six weeks for the ideal length, though longer versions can stretch to eight weeks. The good news is that a fringe trim is quick and cheap — many salons offer it free between cuts. If you are growing them out, ask your stylist to trim only the side pieces every six to eight weeks and leave the centre alone, so the bangs blend into layers instead of hanging in your eyes.

### Can I get curtain bangs with curly hair?

Yes — curly curtain bangs are one of the most flattering versions, but the cut must be done dry, curl by curl, and left at least 2 cm longer than the target length, because curls spring up as they dry. Style with curl cream and a diffuser, and never brush the bangs dry. Once the cut is right, curly curtain bangs are the lowest-maintenance version, because the curls create the shape for you.

### What is the difference between curtain bangs and regular bangs?

Curtain bangs are parted in the middle and longer at the sides, so they frame the face like curtains and blend into the rest of the hair. Regular bangs are cut straight across the forehead at one length and sit flat. Curtain bangs grow out far more gracefully, need less daily styling, and suit more face shapes — which is why they have stayed popular while straight-across bangs come and go.

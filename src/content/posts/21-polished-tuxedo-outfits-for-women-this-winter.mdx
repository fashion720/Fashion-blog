A tuxedo is the most confident thing a woman can wear in winter, and it has come a long way from the borrowed-from-the-boys suit. The modern women's tuxedo is cut for a woman's body — nipped at the waist, shaped at the shoulder — and it works for far more than black tie. This list takes you from the classic black suit through velvet, white, burgundy, and plaid, and from the boardroom to the wedding, the gala, and the holiday party.

Each of the 21 outfits below gives you the exact pieces, the order to put them on, and a formula you can copy. You will learn what to wear under the jacket, which lapel suits your frame, which shoes keep the line clean, and how to make one tuxedo do five different jobs. If you own one suit already, start there; if you are shopping, the checklist at the end tells you what to look for.

## 1. The Classic Black Tuxedo with a White Shirt

The classic black tuxedo with a crisp white shirt is the look every other outfit in this list is measured against. The jacket should have satin peak lapels, a single button, and a shoulder that sits exactly on your shoulder — not beyond it. The trousers should be high-waisted with a satin stripe down the side, falling clean to the shoe with no pooling at the ankle.

Wear the shirt with the collar open and no tie, add black pointed-toe heels and a small black clutch, and keep jewellery to diamond or crystal studs. This is the outfit for black tie, galas, and formal dinners, and it is the one to buy first if you are investing in a single tuxedo. The satin lapel is what makes it a tuxedo rather than a work suit, so never skip it.

Formula: black tuxedo jacket with satin lapels + black tuxedo trousers + white tuxedo shirt + black pointed-toe heels + small black clutch.

Tip: buy the jacket and trousers together and dry-clean them together, always — washing them separately fades the black unevenly and the set stops matching.

## 2. The Black Tuxedo with a Silk Camisole

Swap the shirt for a black silk camisole and the same black tuxedo turns from formal to evening in one move. The camisole softens the neckline, shows a little skin, and lets the satin lapel do the talking without a collar competing with it. Choose a camisole with a lace trim if you want romance, or a plain silk one if you want it sleek.

Add strappy black heels and a metallic clutch, with drop earrings as the only jewellery. Wear the jacket open so the camisole shows, and button it only for photographs. This is the outfit for cocktail parties, work dinners that turn into drinks, and any evening where a full shirt would feel too stiff. For more formal evening ideas, our [16 elegant winter wedding guest outfits](/posts/16-elegant-winter-wedding-guest-outfits-for-women/) use the same open-jacket formula.

Formula: black tuxedo jacket + black tuxedo trousers + black silk camisole + strappy black heels + metallic clutch.

Tip: a camisole under a tuxedo must stay tucked — a camisole that rides up when you sit will spend the whole evening being pulled back into place.

## 3. The Velvet Burgundy Tuxedo for Holiday Parties

A velvet burgundy tuxedo is the holiday party suit, and velvet is the fabric that makes winter evenings feel special. The pile catches the light as you move, the burgundy reads rich without needing sequins, and the warmth of velvet is a real advantage on a December night. Choose a shawl lapel in satin rather than velvet — the contrast between the two textures is what makes the jacket look tailored instead of soft all over.

Wear it with a black silk camisole, black pointed-toe heels, and a small black clutch. Keep jewellery gold and minimal. This is the outfit for Christmas parties, New Year's Eve, and any festive night where a dress would leave you cold. Velvet shows pressure marks, so hang the jacket and never fold it into a suitcase.

Formula: burgundy velvet tuxedo jacket + burgundy velvet trousers + black silk camisole + black pointed-toe heels + small black clutch.

Tip: velvet tuxedos need a lint roller before you leave — the pile picks up every fibre in your wardrobe, and it shows under party lighting.

## 4. The White Tuxedo with Black Trousers

A white tuxedo jacket with black tuxedo trousers is the most striking combination in this list, and it works because the contrast is deliberate. The white jacket is the statement, the black trousers are the anchor, and a black camisole underneath ties the two halves together. Choose an ivory-white rather than a stark white if your skin is warm-toned — stark white can wash you out under indoor lighting.

Add black pointed-toe heels and a black clutch, and keep the jacket buttoned for the most formal version. This is the outfit for winter weddings, evening receptions, and any event where you want to be remembered. White jackets mark easily, so carry a stain pen and avoid red wine until the photographs are done.

Formula: white tuxedo jacket with satin lapels + black tuxedo trousers + black camisole + black pointed-toe heels + black clutch.

Tip: the white jacket must be lined and opaque — an unlined white jacket shows the camisole seams through the back, and that ruins the whole look.

## 5. The Midnight Blue Tuxedo with a Bow Tie

A midnight blue tuxedo with a slim black bow tie is the most traditional black-tie look a woman can wear, and midnight reads blacker than black under evening light. The bow tie is the detail that makes it deliberate rather than borrowed — wear it small and neat, not oversized, and keep the shirt collar crisp so it sits flat underneath.

Add black patent loafers or pointed-toe heels and a black clutch. This is the outfit for the most formal events on your calendar: charity galas, award ceremonies, and strict black-tie weddings. If the invitation says black tie and you do not want a gown, this is the answer. The bow tie works best on women with shorter hair or hair pulled back, so the neckline stays clean.

Formula: midnight blue tuxedo jacket + midnight blue trousers + white tuxedo shirt + slim black bow tie + black patent loafers.

Tip: practice tying the bow tie at home before the event — a crooked bow tie is the first thing anyone notices, and it takes three tries to get it straight.

## 6. The Black Tuxedo with Nothing Underneath

A black tuxedo jacket buttoned over bare skin is the boldest look in this list, and the tailoring is what makes it work instead of reading as a wardrobe accident. The jacket must fit perfectly — nipped at the waist, closed without pulling, and long enough to cover the hip. A single button at the waist and a deep V neckline create the shape, and the trousers stay classic so the jacket carries the drama.

Add pointed-toe heels and a small clutch, with one strong piece of jewellery — a cuff or a pair of drop earrings, never both. This is a red-carpet look, a fashion party look, and a look for women who are comfortable being the most talked-about person in the room. Wear fashion tape at the neckline so the jacket stays exactly where you put it.

Formula: black tuxedo jacket worn buttoned with nothing underneath + black tuxedo trousers + pointed-toe heels + small clutch.

Tip: this only works with a jacket that closes without a gap — if you can see skin pulling at the button, the jacket is too small and the look fails.

Choose a slip dress that falls below the knee so the jacket and dress lengths work together.

Formula: black tuxedo jacket + black silk slip dress + strappy heels + small clutch.

### Your Tuxedo Buying and Fitting Checklist

Before you buy, or before you wear the suit you already own, run through this list:

- **Satin lapels, always** — the satin or grosgrain lapel is what makes a suit a tuxedo; without it, you own a black suit.
- **Shoulders sit exactly on yours** — a shoulder that extends past yours looks borrowed, not tailored.
- **Jacket closes without pulling** — a tuxedo jacket that only closes over a thin top is not a winter jacket.
- **Trousers fall clean to the shoe** — no pooling at the ankle, no flashing the ankle unless the cut is cropped on purpose.
- **Buy the set together** — jacket and trousers from the same fabric batch, dry-cleaned together, so the black always matches.
- **One shine at a time** — satin lapels plus sequins plus metallic shoes is one shine too many; pick one.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the difference between a tuxedo and a suit?

A tuxedo has satin or grosgrain lapels, satin-covered buttons, and usually a satin stripe down the trouser leg, while a regular suit has the same fabric throughout. Those satin details are what make a tuxedo formal eveningwear rather than workwear. A black suit can go to the office; a black tuxedo belongs at black tie, weddings, and evening events.

### Can women wear a tuxedo to a wedding?

Yes, and it is one of the strongest wedding-guest choices, especially for evening or city weddings. A black or jewel-tone tuxedo with a silk camisole reads as formal without competing with the bridal party. Avoid white or ivory unless the suit is clearly tailored suiting rather than a dress, and always check the dress code first.

### What do you wear under a women's tuxedo jacket?

A white tuxedo shirt is the most formal choice, a silk camisole is the most versatile for evenings, and a fine turtleneck is the warmest for winter. A lace camisole softens the look, a sequin top makes it a party outfit, and nothing underneath is the boldest option — but only if the jacket fits perfectly and you use fashion tape.

### What shoes go with a women's tuxedo?

Pointed-toe heels are the classic choice for evening, strappy heels suit cocktail events, and black patent loafers work for the most traditional black-tie looks. For daytime or office versions, a polished loafer or a low block heel keeps it grounded. The shoe should be black or nude — a coloured shoe competes with the suit.

### How should a women's tuxedo fit?

The jacket shoulder should sit exactly on your shoulder, the jacket should close without pulling over whatever you wear underneath, and the waist should be nipped enough to show your shape without straining. Trousers should be high-waisted with a clean line to the shoe. If the jacket fits at the shoulders but not the waist, buy for the shoulders and have the waist tailored — shoulders are expensive to fix, waists are not.

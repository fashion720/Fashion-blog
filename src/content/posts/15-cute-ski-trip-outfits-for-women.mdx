A ski trip is the one holiday where your outfit has to work twice as hard — it needs to keep you warm on the mountain and still look good in every photo. The best ski trip outfits do both: technical layers that actually perform, styled with colours, textures and accessories that feel intentional instead of thrown together.

In this guide, you will find 15 cute ski trip outfits for women, covering everything from your first morning on the slopes to fireside drinks at the lodge. Each look comes with a step-by-step styling guide and a simple outfit formula you can copy, plus practical tips on layering, packing and staying warm without looking bulky. For more ideas on how editors style the same pieces, take a look at [WhoWhatWear's guide to ski outfits](https://www.whowhatwear.com/ski-outfits) alongside this list.

## 1. The Classic Black Ski Suit

A black ski suit is the easiest way to look polished on the slopes. Start with a moisture-wicking thermal base layer in black or charcoal, then add a fitted black ski jacket with a high collar and a matching pair of black ski pants. The monochrome base makes the whole outfit look sleek, and it hides snow, scuffs and chairlift marks better than any pale colour.

Add warmth with a thin merino neck gaiter instead of a bulky scarf, and choose black gloves with touchscreen fingertips so you can check the trail map on your phone. A matte black helmet and mirrored goggles finish the look without breaking the colour story.

Formula: black thermal base layer + black ski jacket + black ski pants + black helmet + mirrored goggles.

Tip: Try the full suit on at home with your boots and helmet before you pack — a jacket that rides up or pants that bunch at the ankle will annoy you all day on the mountain.

## 2. Cream and Camel Après-Ski Knit Set

This is the outfit you change into the moment you hand back your skis. Start with a cream chunky knit sweater, slightly oversized, and pair it with camel wide-leg knit trousers or soft fleece-lined leggings. The cream-and-camel palette looks expensive and photographs beautifully against snow and wooden lodge interiors.

Layer a long camel wool coat over the top for the walk to dinner, and add shearling-lined boots in tan.

Formula: cream chunky knit sweater + camel knit trousers + camel wool coat + tan shearling boots + cream pompom beanie.

Tip: Pack knit sets instead of separate pieces — a matching set takes up less room in your suitcase and always looks put together, even after a long day on the slopes.

![Woman in a cream chunky knit sweater and camel trousers outside a snowy ski lodge](/images/content/15-cute-ski-trip-outfits-for-women/2.webp)

## 3. The Retro Colour-Block Ski Jacket

Retro colour-block ski jackets are having a moment, and they are the easiest way to stand out in a sea of black parkas. Look for a jacket in a bold combination — think teal and cream, red and navy, or mustard and burgundy — with a slightly cropped, boxy fit. Pair it with plain black or navy ski pants so the jacket stays the hero of the outfit.

Underneath, wear a striped thermal top in one of the jacket's colours and a thin fleece mid-layer for insulation. Match your beanie to the brightest stripe in the jacket, and choose goggles with a coloured frame that picks up a secondary colour.

Formula: colour-block ski jacket + black ski pants + striped thermal top + matching beanie + coloured-frame goggles.

Tip: When you buy a bold jacket, check that the zips, pockets and vents are genuinely waterproof — vintage-look styles sometimes trade performance for looks, and wet pockets ruin a ski day fast.

![Woman in a retro teal and cream colour-block ski jacket on the ski slopes](/images/content/15-cute-ski-trip-outfits-for-women/3.webp)

## 4. White Puffer and Burgundy Base Layers

White outerwear looks fresh and striking on snow, and burgundy underneath keeps it from feeling washed out. Start with a burgundy thermal base layer and burgundy ski pants, then add a white puffer ski jacket with a cinched waist or belted detail. The contrast between the crisp white shell and the deep wine tones underneath is what makes this look work.

Add a burgundy beanie and matching gloves, and let a burgundy neck gaiter peek out at the collar.

Formula: burgundy thermal base layer + burgundy ski pants + white puffer ski jacket + burgundy beanie and gloves.

Tip: White ski jackets show dirt quickly, so carry a small stain wipe in your jacket pocket and avoid sitting directly on chairlift seats — brush the snow off first.

![Woman in a white puffer ski jacket with burgundy ski pants on snowy slopes](/images/content/15-cute-ski-trip-outfits-for-women/4.webp)

## 5. The Fair Isle Sweater and Ski Pants Combo

A Fair Isle sweater is the cosiest way to bring a classic alpine feel to your ski trip outfits. Wear a Fair Isle knit in cream, navy and red under an open ski jacket on milder days, or as your lodge layer once you are off the slopes. Pair it with black ski pants and sturdy snow boots for a look that works from the lift queue to the hot chocolate stand.

Keep the rest simple: dark pants, plain gloves and a solid-colour beanie. A small crossbody bag in tan leather is handy for your lift pass, phone and lip balm, and it looks far cuter than a bulky backpack.

Formula: Fair Isle knit sweater + black ski pants + tan snow boots + solid beanie + tan crossbody bag.

Tip: Fair Isle knits pill easily under backpack straps, so wear your bag crossbody or carry it in your hand on the walk to the lodge.

## 6. Pastel Pink Ski Suit

Pastel pink is the softest way to do a full ski suit, and it looks gorgeous in mountain photos. Choose a blush or baby-pink ski suit with a slightly glossy finish, and layer a white thermal underneath. The pale pink reads as a neutral in snowy light, so it pairs easily with white, cream and silver accessories.

Add a white faux-fur-trimmed beanie, white gloves and a white helmet if you can find one, or keep the helmet pink for a full monochrome moment. Silver mirrored goggles add a cool, modern edge that stops the pink from feeling too sweet.

Formula: pastel pink ski suit + white thermal base layer + white faux-fur beanie + white gloves + silver mirrored goggles.

Tip: Pastel suits can look thin in flat light, so choose one with a matte finish and sealed seams — cheap shiny pastels crease and show every fold in photos.

## 7. The Plaid Flannel and Faux-Fur Vest Lodge Look

Some of the best ski trip moments happen off the slopes, and this lodge look is made for them. Start with a red and black buffalo plaid flannel shirt, half-tucked into dark skinny jeans or fleece-lined leggings. Layer a cream faux-fur vest over the top for warmth and texture, and add tan suede ankle boots with a low heel.

A wide-brim camel felt hat or a chunky knit beanie both work — the hat for a village stroll, the beanie for colder evenings. Plaid works far beyond the slopes, too — [Who What Wear's plaid outfits](https://www.whowhatwear.com/fashion/fall/plaid-fall-and-winter-womens-outfit-ideas) shows five fresh ways to style the pattern for fall and winter.

Formula: buffalo plaid flannel shirt + dark jeans or leggings + cream faux-fur vest + tan suede ankle boots.

Tip: Faux-fur vests shed in the wash, so spot-clean yours and hang it to air out between wears.

## 8. Metallic Silver Statement Jacket

If you want one outfit that turns heads on the lift, make it metallic. A silver ski jacket with a subtle shine catches the light beautifully and looks futuristic against white snow and blue sky. Pair it with black ski pants and a black base layer so the jacket gets all the attention, and add black gloves and a black helmet for balance.

Keep accessories minimal and cool-toned: silver or clear goggles, a grey beanie and a black neck gaiter. Avoid gold jewellery here — silver tones keep the look cohesive.

Formula: metallic silver ski jacket + black ski pants + black base layer + black helmet + silver mirrored goggles.

Tip: Metallic shells can feel stiff, so look for one with four-way stretch panels at the shoulders — you need full arm movement for poling and turning.

![Woman in a metallic silver ski jacket and black ski pants on the ski slopes](/images/content/15-cute-ski-trip-outfits-for-women/8.webp)

## 9. The Matching Ski Set in Forest Green

Matching ski sets look instantly intentional, and forest green is a rich, flattering choice that suits almost every skin tone. Choose a forest-green jacket and pants in the same fabric and finish, then layer a cream thermal and a cream fleece underneath. The cream peeking at the collar and cuffs softens the deep green and stops the set from looking like a uniform.

Add tan or cognac gloves and a cream beanie for a warm, earthy contrast, and pick goggles with a brown or amber lens to complete the palette. A small tan backpack carries your extras without clashing.

Formula: forest-green ski jacket and pants + cream thermal + cream beanie + tan gloves + amber-lens goggles.

Tip: Buy the jacket and pants as a set from the same brand and season — greens vary a lot between brands, and a mismatched set is easy to spot in photos.

## 10. Chunky Knit Turtleneck and Leather-Look Ski Pants

This look mixes cosy and polished in a way that works for après-ski drinks and village dinners. Start with an oversized chunky knit turtleneck in cream, oatmeal or soft grey, and pair it with black leather-look ski pants or faux-leather leggings. The shine of the pants against the matte knit creates a lovely texture contrast.

Add knee-high boots in black suede or leather, and layer a long black wool coat over everything for the walk outside.

Formula: chunky knit turtleneck + black leather-look ski pants + black knee-high boots + long black wool coat.

Tip: Leather-look pants can feel cold against bare skin, so always wear a thermal legging underneath — you will stay warm and the pants will slide on easily.

## 11. The All-White Monochrome Slope Look

An all-white ski outfit is the boldest choice on this list — and the most photogenic. Wear a white ski jacket and white ski pants with a white thermal underneath, then break the monochrome slightly with cream or ivory accessories: a cream beanie, cream gloves and a cream neck gaiter. The tonal layers keep the look from feeling flat.

Choose goggles with a light blue or rose lens to add a hint of colour, and wear a white helmet to complete the head-to-toe effect.

Formula: white ski jacket + white ski pants + white thermal + cream beanie and gloves + rose-lens goggles.

Tip: All-white outfits show every mark, so pack a second pair of gloves — wet, dirty gloves are the first thing people notice in photos.

## 12. Burgundy and Blush Après-Ski Dress with Boots

For a ski-town dinner or an evening at the lodge bar, swap the ski pants for a dress. Choose a burgundy sweater dress in a cosy knit, just above the knee, and layer blush pink tights or fleece-lined tights underneath. Add knee-high boots in tan or burgundy suede, and a faux-fur stole or a cropped shearling jacket over the top.

Keep jewellery warm-toned — gold hoops and a thin gold chain — and carry a small crossbody bag so your hands stay free for a warm drink. A soft wave in your hair and a berry lip colour finish the look.

Formula: burgundy sweater dress + blush fleece-lined tights + tan knee-high boots + cropped shearling jacket + gold hoops.

Tip: Fleece-lined tights are the secret weapon of winter dressing — they look like sheer tights in photos but feel like leggings, so you can wear a dress in freezing weather comfortably.

## 13. The Oversized Puffer and Leggings Village Stroll

Not every ski trip moment needs full ski gear — sometimes you just want to wander the village, browse shops and grab coffee. Start with black fleece-lined leggings and an oversized puffer jacket in a fun colour like sage green, dusty blue or soft lilac. Layer a cropped hoodie or a chunky knit underneath for extra warmth.

Add chunky snow boots in white or tan, a matching beanie and a crossbody bag worn across the puffer.

Formula: oversized coloured puffer + black fleece-lined leggings + cropped hoodie + chunky snow boots + matching beanie.

Tip: When you wear an oversized puffer, keep everything underneath fitted — bulky layers under a bulky jacket make you look twice your size and restrict movement.

## 14. Red and Navy Vintage-Inspired Ski Look

Channel classic alpine style with a red and navy vintage-inspired ski outfit. Choose a navy ski jacket with red piping or a red jacket with navy panels, and pair it with navy ski pants. The two-tone palette feels timeless and looks wonderful in photos, especially against wooden chalets and pine trees.

Layer a cream Fair Isle or striped thermal underneath, and add a red beanie with a white pompom for a playful vintage touch. Navy gloves and a navy helmet keep the colours balanced, while goggles with a red frame tie the whole look together.

## 15. The Cozy Sherpa and Denim Ski-Town Night Out

End your trip with the cosiest outfit of all. Wear dark straight-leg jeans with a cream sherpa jacket or a sherpa-lined denim jacket, layered over a simple white thermal or a thin turtleneck. The sherpa texture is warm, soft and instantly inviting — perfect for a final night of hot chocolate, live music and lodge-hopping.

Add tan ankle boots with a grippy sole for icy pavements, and a cream beanie to keep the palette soft. A small tan leather bag and simple gold jewellery finish the look without any fuss.

Formula: cream sherpa jacket + dark straight-leg jeans + white thermal + tan ankle boots + cream beanie.

Tip: Sherpa jackets attract lint and snow, so keep a small lint roller in your bag.

## Ski Trip Packing Checklist

Before you zip your suitcase, run through this quick checklist so nothing important gets left behind:

- Thermal base layers (top and bottom) — at least two sets
- A mid-layer fleece or lightweight puffer for insulation
- Ski jacket and pants, plus a waterproof shell
- Warm socks, gloves and a spare pair of gloves
- A beanie, a neck gaiter and a balaclava for very cold days
- Goggles and a helmet (or confirm your rental includes them)
- Après-ski outfits: a knit set, a sweater dress and a lodge look
- Snow boots with good grip, plus one pair of evening boots
- Lip balm with SPF, sunscreen, first-aid kit
- Hand warmers, portable charger, waterproof phone pouch

Pack your bulkiest layers in your bag and wear your ski jacket on the plane — it saves suitcase space and keeps you warm on arrival. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What should I wear on a ski trip if I have never skied before?

Wear a moisture-wicking thermal base layer, a fleece mid-layer and a waterproof ski jacket and pants. Rent your skis, boots, helmet and poles at the resort, and buy gloves, goggles and a beanie before you go. Avoid cotton — it stays wet and makes you cold fast. Dress in layers you can remove as you warm up.

### What do you wear to après-ski?

Après-ski outfits are cosy and relaxed: chunky knit sweaters, fleece-lined leggings, knit sets, sweater dresses with tights, and warm boots. Swap your ski gear for soft layers, add a wool coat or shearling jacket, and keep a beanie on for the walk between venues. Comfort matters more than formality at the lodge.

### How do I stay warm skiing without looking bulky?

Use thin, technical layers instead of one thick coat: a merino thermal, a fitted fleece and a well-insulated ski jacket trap heat without bulk. Choose ski pants with a slim cut, tuck your base layer in, and let accessories like a neck gaiter do the work of a scarf. Heated hand warmers help too.

### Can I wear leggings skiing?

You can wear leggings as a base layer under ski pants, but not on their own — they are not waterproof or windproof enough for the slopes. Choose fleece-lined or thermal leggings under your ski pants for warmth. For village strolls and lodge evenings, leggings with an oversized puffer are perfect.

### What shoes should I pack for a ski trip?

Pack three pairs: ski boots (rented at the resort), waterproof snow boots with a grippy sole for walking around the village, and one smart pair — knee-high boots or suede ankle boots — for dinners and evenings.
